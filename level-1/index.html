How to use this guide

Each day is one sitting of 45–60 minutes. Follow the same routine every day:

2 minWarm-up: answer the three questions from earlier pages at the top of the page.
30–40 minRead the page. Study each diagram before reading the text below it.
10 minCheck yourself: try the fault scenario and the questions before opening the answers.
1 minMark the page as done. Your progress is saved in this browser.

Every page has the same shape, so you always know where to look: After this page you can → In one line → Why it matters → How it works → Follow AHU-01 → On site → Common faults → Key terms → Check yourself. Dotted-underlined abbreviations show their meaning when you hover over or tap them. Green-edged boxes are interactive tools — use them.

Meet AHU-01

Instead of learning each topic in isolation, you will follow one real-world example through the whole level: AHU-01, an air handling unit cooling the 3rd-floor offices of a commercial building. Module by module you will learn its plant, its sensors, its wiring, its communication and its control logic — and in the labs you will produce its complete control package.

Its job: hold the offices at 24 °C from 08:00 to 19:00, Monday to Saturday; bring in enough fresh air for the people inside; stop safely on a fire alarm; and alarm on any failure.

About this guide

This guide is vendor-neutral. It is based on general industry practice and open standards — ISO 16484 (building automation), ASHRAE 135 (BACnet) and the Modbus specification — so it applies whichever brand of BMS you work with. Vendor-specific notes may be added to pages later. On site, always follow the manufacturer's documentation, the project specification and local electrical regulations.
