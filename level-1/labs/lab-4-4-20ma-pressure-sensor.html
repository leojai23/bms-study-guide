Practical lab 4

4–20 mA pressure sensor wiring

Wire, scale, configure and fault-find AHU-01's 4–20 mA duct pressure sensor.

Week 3 · Day 19About 60 min (+ 30 min with hardware)✓ Done

Time

About 60 min (+ 30 min with hardware)

You will produce

A loop drawing, a scaling table, the AI configuration, a test record and answers to six fault cases.

Read first

4.3 Analog signals: 0–10 V, 4–20 mA, 0–20 mA
3.3 Differential pressure and airflow sensors
4.5 Sensor wiring and troubleshooting
4.1 Electrical safety for BMS work

What you need

  • Pen and paper or a drawing tool
  • The scaling calculator below
  • Optional hardware: 24 V DC supply, a 2-wire 4–20 mA transmitter or loop calibrator, a 250 Ω resistor, a multimeter

Tasks

1 · Draw the loop

Draw the 2-wire loop for the duct pressure sensor: 24 V DC supply (G1) with its fuse (F2), transmitter + and −, controller input UI5 set to mA, and the return to 0 V. Add terminal numbers (X1:11, X1:12) and cable C05. Mark where you would connect a meter in series.

2 · Make the scaling table

For a 0–500 Pa transmitter, calculate the current at 0, 100, 250, 400 and 500 Pa, and the pressure for 6, 10 and 18 mA. Check your answers with the calculator.

Interactive tool

3 · Check the voltage budget

The transmitter needs at least 12 V. The controller input has a 250 Ω burden, and the 60 m of 0.75 mm² cable has about 3 Ω. Is 24 V enough? What is the largest burden the loop could take?

4 · Configure the input

Write down the AI settings: signal type, range, units, filtering, and what the controller should do below 3.6 mA and above 21 mA.

5 · Test it

Write a short test procedure: what you measure, where, and the expected values. With hardware: build the loop on the bench with a 250 Ω resistor in place of the controller and measure the voltage across it (1–5 V) as you change the pressure or the calibrator output.

6 · Fault cases

Diagnose each: (a) BMS shows sensor fault, loop current 0 mA; (b) 3.0 mA; (c) 22.5 mA; (d) BMS shows 500 Pa while a hand-held manometer reads 250 Pa; (e) the value drops to zero whenever the panel door is opened; (f) reading correct, but swings ±40 Pa when the VFD speeds up.

Voltage budget

At 20 mA: burden 250 Ω × 0.02 A = 5 V; cable 3 Ω × 0.02 A = 0.06 V. 24 − 5 − 0.06 = 18.9 V across the transmitter, above its 12 V minimum — fine. Largest total loop resistance: (24 − 12) ÷ 0.02 = 600 Ω.

Test procedure

  1. Isolate nothing — this is a 24 V DC loop — but check the panel for other voltages first (page 4.1).
  2. Measure 24 V DC at G1 and after F2.
  3. With the fan off, measure the loop current in series at the test terminal: expect about 4 mA (0 Pa).
  4. Start the fan; compare the BMS value with a hand-held manometer at the same tapping; compare the current with the scaling table.
  5. Open the loop at the test terminal: the BMS must show a sensor fault and raise the alarm.
  6. Record supply voltage, current at rest and running, and the BMS values.

Fault cases

CaseDiagnosis
(a) 0 mAOpen loop or no supply: check F2, 24 V at G1, terminations and cable continuity
(b) 3.0 mABelow the live zero: transmitter failing or under-powered — check voltage across it
(c) 22.5 mAOver-range or failed transmitter; short between cores
(d) 500 Pa vs 250 PaRange mismatch: transmitter set to 0–250 Pa while the BMS scales 0–500 Pa (or the reverse)
(e) zero when the door opensLoose termination or a cable strained by the door hinge
(f) swings with VFD speedNoise pick-up — cable near the motor cable or screen not earthed; or real turbulence at the tapping
