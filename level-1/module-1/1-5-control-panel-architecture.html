In one line

A BMS control panel (DDC panel) is the enclosure that houses the controller with its power supplies, protection, relays and terminals, so field wiring can be connected safely and neatly.

?Why it matters

Most hands-on BMS work happens at the panel: terminating cables, measuring signals, replacing relays, connecting a laptop. A well-built panel makes this quick and safe; a poor one hides faults behind loose terminals and missing labels. You also need to read panel drawings to wire, test and fault-find.

Why interposing relays?

A controller's digital output is a small relay or triac meant for low currents. A motor starter in the MCC may work at 230 V and draw more current, and a fault in that circuit could destroy the controller. An interposing relay sits between them: the controller's output energises the relay coil at 24 V, and the relay's own, stronger contact switches the field circuit.

  1. Isolation — the 230 V field circuit never reaches the controller.
  2. Higher rating — the relay contact can carry more current and voltage than the controller output.
  3. Easy replacement — plug-in relays are cheap and swapped in seconds; a damaged controller output is not.
  4. Visible state — most relays have an LED, so you can see the command without a meter.

The BMS–MCC interface

Fans and pumps are powered and protected in the motor control centre (MCC) or a starter panel — not in the BMS panel. The two panels exchange a few simple signals:

SignalDirectionPoint typeMeaning
Start / stop commandBMS → MCCDO (relay contact)Contact closed = run
Run statusMCC → BMSDIContactor auxiliary or VFD relay closed = running
Trip / faultMCC → BMSDIOverload tripped or VFD fault
Auto / manual statusMCC → BMSDISelector in Auto = the BMS is in control
Speed referenceBMS → VFDAO (0–10 V or 4–20 mA)Required fan or pump speed

These contacts are potential-free (also called volt-free or dry): each one is just a switch, and the receiving side supplies its own voltage. That keeps the two panels electrically independent. Module 4 explains this fully.

The HOA switch

Hand–Off–Auto. In Hand the motor runs locally whatever the BMS says; in Off it is stopped; only in Auto does the BMS command it. Many “the BMS won't start the fan” calls end at a switch left in Hand or Off — which is exactly why the auto/manual status is wired back to the BMS.

Good panel practice

  • Segregate 230 V wiring from 24 V signal wiring — separate trunking and terminal groups. Mixing them causes electrical noise and is a safety risk.
  • Ferrule every wire end with a number that matches the drawing, and label every device.
  • Keep 10–20 % spare terminals, I/O and DIN-rail space.
  • Earth cable screens at one end only, normally at the panel (more in Module 4).
  • Choose an enclosure IP rating for the location — e.g. IP54 in a plant room, higher outdoors.
  • Keep a copy of the drawings in the door pocket and update them after every change.

Panels are tested twice: a factory acceptance test (FAT) at the panel builder, and a site acceptance test after installation, when every point is checked end to end.

Follow AHU-01

Panel DDC-AHU-01 sits in the 3rd-floor AHU room. It takes 230 V from the floor's distribution board through a 6 A MCB and contains: a 230/24 V AC transformer for the controller and the two actuators; a 24 V DC supply for the 4–20 mA duct pressure sensor; one interposing relay for the fan start; terminals for all hard-wired points plus spares; and RS485 terminals for the Modbus link to the VFD.

⚒On site

  • Before working inside a panel, isolate it and prove it dead with a tester. The incoming terminals stay live even when the controller is off.
  • Measure the 24 V AC at the transformer and at the controller while the actuators are moving — that is when a weak supply shows.
  • Tug each wire gently to find loose terminals; they cause intermittent faults that are hard to trace.
  • Relay LED on but the motor does not run? The problem is on the MCC side: HOA position, contactor, overload, wiring.
  • Mark up the drawings when you change wiring. Up-to-date “as-built” drawings save the next engineer hours.

AKey terms

DDC panel
Enclosure holding the controller, power supplies, protection, relays and terminals.
Control transformer
Steps 230 V AC down to 24 V AC for controllers and actuators; rated in VA.
SMPS
Switched-mode power supply — here, the 24 V DC supply for sensors.
Interposing relay
A relay between a controller output and a field circuit, for isolation and higher rating.
Potential-free contact
A contact that is only a switch, carrying no voltage of its own. Also “volt-free” or “dry”.
MCC
Motor control centre — where motors are powered, started and protected.
HOA switch
Hand–Off–Auto selector; the BMS has control only in Auto.
Ferrule
A numbered marker on a wire end matching the drawing.
Segregation
Keeping power and signal wiring physically separate.
FAT
Factory acceptance test of the panel before delivery.
IP rating
Ingress Protection — how well an enclosure keeps out dust and water (e.g. IP54).

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

The BMS commands AHU-01's fan ON. The interposing relay's LED is lit, but the fan is not running. The auto/manual status on the graphic shows “Manual”. What is the likely cause?

The relay LED proves the BMS is commanding the fan, and the auto/manual status says the MCC is not in Auto. Put the switch back to Auto — and find out who left it in Hand or Off, and why.

  1. Why are field cables landed on terminal blocks rather than directly on the controller?

    Show answer
    Terminal blocks give a clear, numbered connection point for testing and disconnection, protect the controller's terminals from repeated work, and let the controller be replaced without disturbing field wiring.

  2. Give three reasons for using interposing relays.

    Show answer
    Isolation of the controller from 230 V circuits, higher contact rating, cheap and quick replacement, and a visible LED showing the command.

  3. What does “potential-free contact” mean?

    Show answer
    The contact is only a switch and carries no voltage from its own panel; the receiving panel supplies the voltage. It keeps the two panels electrically independent.

  4. List the signals usually exchanged between the BMS and a fan's MCC starter.

    Show answer
    Start/stop command (DO), run status (DI), trip/fault (DI), auto/manual status (DI), and a speed reference (AO) if a VFD is fitted.

  5. Why should 230 V and 24 V signal wiring be kept apart?

    Show answer
    For safety, and because power cables induce electrical noise into signal cables, which makes analogue readings unstable.
