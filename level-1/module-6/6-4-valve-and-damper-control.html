Module 6 · BMS Control Logic

6.4 Temperature-based valve control and damper control

Week 4 · Day 27About 10 minPage 4 of 6✓ Done

  • Explain cascade control and supply air temperature reset.
  • Use split-range control with a deadband to avoid simultaneous heating and cooling.

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. Why does P-only control leave an offset?
    from 6.3 PID control basics
    Show answer
    It needs an error to produce any output; to hold a non-zero output it must settle with a steady error.
  2. Give two control (software) interlocks for an AHU.
    from 6.2 Fan interlocking and safeties
    Show answer
    Any two of: valve closed when fan off; return fan follows supply fan; damper proven open before fan start; make-up air with exhaust.
  3. Why close the CHW valve when the fan stops?
    from 6.1 AHU start/stop sequence
    Show answer
    A cold coil without airflow cools the casing and condenses water — sweating, dripping and wasted chilled water.
In one line

Temperature loops drive the valves, air-quality and economy logic drive the dampers — and simple min/max selects let limits and overrides take over safely when needed.

?Why it matters

Real AHUs run several loops at once, and those loops interact. Knowing the standard patterns — cascade, reset, split range, minimum position, select logic — lets you read any vendor's program, write clear sequences and spot why two loops are fighting each other.

Combining loops: min and max select

When two loops want to drive the same output, a select decides. A min select takes the lower of the two outputs: AHU-01's valve = min(return-air loop, supply-air low-limit loop), so the limit can only reduce cooling. A max select takes the higher: fresh air damper = max(minimum position, CO₂ demand, economiser), so ventilation can never fall below the minimum.

Cascade control and supply air reset

  • Cascade: an outer room (or return) loop calculates a supply air setpoint; an inner supply air loop drives the valve to it. The inner loop catches disturbances — such as a change in chilled water temperature — before the room notices.
  • Supply air temperature reset: in VAV systems, the supply air setpoint is raised (e.g. from 13 °C towards 16 °C) when zones need little cooling, saving chiller energy — but limited by humidity, because warmer coil air removes less moisture.

Split range with a deadband

Where an AHU has both heating and cooling, one PID output can drive both: the bottom of the range opens the heating valve, the top opens the cooling valve, and a deadband in the middle keeps both closed. This makes simultaneous heating and cooling impossible — a common, expensive fault when two separate loops are used.

Valves on FCUs and other small units

On/off (two-position) valves use a thermostat with a deadband and often a minimum on/off time rather than PID (page 2.3). Modulating FCU valves use the same PI principles as AHUs, usually inside an application-specific controller with pre-set tuning.

Follow AHU-01

In code-like terms: CHWV = min(PID_RAT, PID_SAT_lowlimit) when the fan is proven, else 0 %; FAD = max(20 %, DCV(CO₂)) limited to 50 % when the fan is proven, else 0 %. Two lines, four loops, every case covered — that is what good control logic looks like.

⚒On site

  • Trend every loop that drives the same output together — fighting loops show up immediately.
  • Check select logic by forcing one input: e.g. warm the supply air sensor's limit and watch the valve.
  • For complementary dampers, check both move — and in opposite directions — across the full range.
  • Look for heating and cooling valves open at the same time; it is never intentional.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

In a meeting room AHU with both heating and cooling, trends show the heating valve at 30 % and the cooling valve at 45 % for hours. Each valve has its own PID loop with the same setpoint. What is the fix?

Two loops chasing the same setpoint end up fighting, heating and cooling at once. Split range with a deadband makes simultaneous operation impossible.

  1. Why is AHU-01's valve a min select of two loops?

    Show answer
    So the supply air low-limit loop can reduce cooling when supply air gets too cold, while the return air loop controls normally otherwise.

  2. Why is the fresh air damper a max select?

    Show answer
    So ventilation never falls below the minimum, whatever the CO₂ or economiser logic asks for.

  3. What is cascade control?

    Show answer
    An outer loop (room or return temperature) sets the setpoint of an inner loop (supply air), which drives the valve.

  4. What does the deadband in split-range control prevent?

    Show answer
    Heating and cooling being active at the same time.

  5. Why is an economiser less useful in hot, humid climates?

    Show answer
    Outdoor air is rarely both cooler and drier than return air, so its enthalpy is usually higher.
