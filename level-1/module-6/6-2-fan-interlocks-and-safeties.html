6.2 Fan interlocking and safeties

In one line

Interlocks make one thing depend on another; safety interlocks are hard-wired so they stop plant even if the BMS, its program or its network fails — and even when the plant is in Hand.

?Why it matters

Interlocks protect people, ductwork and equipment. A fire signal that only reaches the BMS program is not a safety function — software can crash, be overridden, or be bypassed by a switch in Hand. Knowing which interlocks must be hard-wired, and where they go, is a basic responsibility of a BMS engineer.

The fire alarm interface

  • The fire strategy (a fire engineer's document) says what each fan does in a fire: stop, or — for smoke-extract and stair-pressurisation fans — run. The BMS follows it; it does not decide it.
  • The fire system's relay contact is wired fail-safe (closed when healthy) into the starter circuit upstream of the HOA switch.
  • A second contact goes to a BMS DI so the BMS shows Fire shutdown, closes valves and dampers, and raises a critical alarm.
  • Fans that must run in fire mode are controlled by the fire system with priority over the BMS — never the other way round.

Resets

Safety trips usually latch: plant stays off until a person resets it after finding the cause — a manual reset button on a pressure cut-out, an operator reset on the BMS after a fan failure. Automatic restart is reserved for conditions where restarting is clearly safe, such as power returning.

Never bypass a safety to “get it running”

Links across fire contacts or pressure cut-outs, left in “temporarily”, remove protection permanently and invisibly. If a safety stops the plant, find out why.

Testing interlocks

  1. Test each interlock by operating the real device — the fire relay, the pressure switch, the overload trip — not just by forcing a BMS point.
  2. Test in Auto and in Hand: safeties must stop the fan in both.
  3. Check the BMS indication and alarm for each trip.
  4. Check the reset: the plant must not restart until reset, and must restart correctly after it.
  5. Record each test — fire interface tests are usually witnessed.
Follow AHU-01

AHU-01's starter circuit has, in series and before the HOA switch: the fire interface relay (closed when healthy), a high static pressure cut-out on the supply duct (manual reset), and the VFD's healthy contact. The BMS monitors the fire signal, the VFD fault and the auto/manual status on DIs. Software interlocks: valve closed and damper closed whenever the fan is not proven.

⚒On site

  • Trace the fire interface wiring yourself — check it sits upstream of the HOA switch.
  • Look for links or jumpers across safety terminals; report any you find.
  • Find out the fire strategy for every fan before writing fire-mode logic.
  • After a fire alarm test, check every AHU stopped — and restarted afterwards as specified.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

During a witnessed fire alarm test, AHU-03 stops in Auto, but when the technician switches its HOA to Hand, the fan restarts while the fire alarm is still active. What is wrong?

Safety contacts must be in series upstream of the HOA switch so they act in every mode. Rewire it and repeat the test.

  1. Why must a fire shutdown be hard-wired?

    Show answer
    So it works even if the BMS, its program or network fails, and cannot be overridden by software or bypassed by Hand mode.

  2. Where in the starter circuit do safety contacts go?

    Show answer
    In series, upstream of the HOA switch, so they stop the fan in Hand and Auto.

  3. Give two control (software) interlocks for an AHU.

    Show answer
    Any two of: valve closed when fan off; return fan follows supply fan; damper proven open before fan start; make-up air with exhaust.

  4. Who decides what each fan does in a fire?

    Show answer
    The fire strategy, written by the fire engineer; the BMS follows it.

  5. Why are most safety trips latching?

    Show answer
    So plant stays off until a person has found and fixed the cause, rather than cycling on and off into a fault.
