In one line

A start/stop sequence starts plant in a safe order, proves each step, and stops it cleanly — with a defined response when anything fails along the way.

?Why it matters

Starting a fan against a closed damper, opening a valve with no airflow, or restarting forty AHUs at once after a power cut all cause real damage or trips. A good sequence prevents them, and the failure paths in it are what turn a silent fault into a clear alarm. This is the logic you will write and test in Lab 3.

⚙How it works

The start sequence

  1. Open the fresh air damper to minimum (20 %). A fan should not start against a closed intake.
  2. Start the fan. DO on (via the interposing relay); the VFD ramps to its 42 Hz speed setpoint over about 30 s.
  3. Prove it. Run status must arrive within 30 s. If not: “fan fail” alarm, command off, damper closed, lock out until reset.
  4. Stabilise (60 s). The valve stays at 0 % while airflow settles — PID loops started too early react to start-up transients.
  5. Enable control. Start the RAT PID loop (bumpless, from 0 %), and CO₂ control of the damper.
  6. Arm the temperature alarms after 15 min, so the morning pull-down does not raise high-temperature alarms.

The stop sequence

  1. Disable the PID loop and drive the CHW valve to 0 % — a cold coil without airflow sweats and drips.
  2. Command the fan off. Run status must clear within 60 s, or raise “fan failed to stop” (welded contactor, drive in Hand).
  3. Close the fresh air damper (spring-return closes it anyway on power loss).
  4. Start the minimum off timer.

Follow AHU-01

This page is AHU-01's sequence. Use the stepper above: tick “fan fails to prove” and step through, then reset and try “fire” halfway through the day. Notice that in both cases the plant ends in a safe, alarmed, locked-out state — the goal of every failure path.

⚒On site

  • Test the sequence with real failures, not only simulated points: open the status contact, put the HOA in Hand, operate the fire relay.
  • Time the proving and stabilising delays with a stopwatch against the specification.
  • After a power-return test, check every AHU restarted — and in the planned order.
  • Write the as-left delays and setpoints into the commissioning record.

AKey terms

Permissive
Condition that must be true before plant may start.
Proving delay
Maximum time allowed for status to confirm a command.
Stabilising delay
Wait after start before enabling control loops.
Lockout
State after a failure where plant stays off until reset.
Bumpless transfer
Starting or switching a loop without a sudden jump in output.
Minimum off time
Shortest time plant must stay off before restarting (anti-short-cycle).
Staggered restart
Restarting plant items one after another after a power return.
Timed override
Temporary occupied mode, e.g. 2 h after hours.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

Every morning AHU-01 raises “fan fail” and locks out, but restarts fine when reset. The VFD ramp-up time was recently changed to 45 s. What is wrong?

Depending on how the drive's run relay is set, status may only appear near full speed. Set the proving delay longer than the ramp (e.g. 60 s), or use a relay function that closes as soon as the drive runs.

  1. Why is the fresh air damper opened before the fan starts?

    Show answer
    So the fan does not start against a closed intake, which can over-pressurise or collapse ductwork and stress the fan.

  2. What happens if run status is not received within the proving delay?

    Show answer
    “Fan fail” alarm, start command off, damper closed, and the AHU locks out until an operator resets it.

  3. Why wait 60 s before enabling the PID loop?

    Show answer
    To let airflow and readings settle; a loop enabled during start-up transients overreacts.

  4. Why close the CHW valve when the fan stops?

    Show answer
    A cold coil without airflow cools the casing and condenses water — sweating, dripping and wasted chilled water.

  5. What is a staggered restart?

    Show answer
    Restarting plant items one after another after power returns, so their starting currents do not overload the supply or generator.
