5.1 RS485 communication basics

In one line

RS485 is the electrical standard for the twisted-pair cable that BACnet MS/TP and Modbus RTU run on — a daisy chain of devices, terminated at both ends, sending data as a voltage difference between two wires.

?Why it matters

Most field-bus problems in buildings are not protocol problems at all — they are wiring problems: a spur, a missing terminator, swapped wires, a shared earth. RS485 is forgiving when wired properly and miserable when not. Getting the physical layer right first saves hours of chasing settings.

⚙How it works

What RS485 is

RS485 (formally TIA/EIA-485) defines only the electrical signals: how ones and zeros are sent as a voltage difference between two wires. It says nothing about addresses or messages — that is the job of the protocol running on top, such as Modbus RTU or BACnet MS/TP. In BMS it is used half-duplex on two wires: all devices share one pair and take turns to talk.

Because the receiver looks at the difference between the two wires, noise that hits both wires equally cancels out. That is why the pair must be twisted.

The rules

RuleWhy
Daisy chain only — in and out of each deviceStars and long spurs cause signal reflections
120 Ω terminator at each end of the bus, nowhere elseMatches the cable and stops reflections; extra terminators overload the drivers
Bias (fail-safe) resistors at one point, usually the masterHold the line in a defined idle state when nobody is talking
Use a common (0 V / reference) wire as well as A and BKeeps all devices' ground potentials close; prevents damage and errors
Twisted-pair cable of about 120 Ω impedance, screenedNoise rejection and correct termination
Earth the screen at one pointAvoids earth loops
Keep away from power and VFD motor cablesNoise

Limits

LimitTypical valueNotes

A and B naming

Manufacturers label the two wires differently — A/B, +/−, D+/D−, Data+/Data− — and some use “A” for the opposite wire from others. Follow each device's own manual. If a single device will not talk, trying the pair the other way round is a legitimate test: reversed polarity does not damage RS485 transceivers.

Follow AHU-01

DDC-AHU-01 has one RS485 port to the VFD — a short, two-device bus. It still needs a terminator at each end (one in the controller, one in the VFD, both switchable), bias at the controller, a common wire, and screened twisted pair kept apart from the VFD's motor cable in the AHU room.

⚒On site

  • Draw the bus as installed — device by device — before fault-finding. Hidden spurs and stars appear immediately.
  • Look for terminator switches or jumpers on every device; only the two end devices should have them on.
  • Check biasing: some devices have it built in, some not; usually one point should provide it.
  • Never connect the screen to device terminals at both ends “to be safe”.

AKey terms

RS485
Electrical standard for differential, multi-drop serial communication on a twisted pair.
Half-duplex
Devices share one pair and take turns to transmit.
Daisy chain
Topology where the cable runs from device to device in a line.
Termination
120 Ω resistor at each end of the bus to stop reflections.
Biasing
Resistors holding the bus in a defined idle state.
Unit load
Measure of how much a device loads the bus; 32 standard unit loads per segment.
Repeater
Device that starts a new RS485 segment, extending length and device count.
Common (reference) wire
Third conductor linking the devices' signal grounds.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

On a new MS/TP bus, the first 12 FCUs communicate but the last 8 never appear. Power is fine at all of them. What do you check first?

On a daisy chain, everything past a break is lost together. Check continuity and polarity at the joint after the last working device. (Settings faults would usually affect devices individually.)

  1. What does RS485 define, and what does it not?

    Show answer
    It defines the electrical signalling only. It does not define addresses or messages — those come from the protocol on top (Modbus RTU, BACnet MS/TP).

  2. Where are 120 Ω terminators fitted?

    Show answer
    At the two physical ends of the bus only.

  3. What should the A–B resistance be with the power off and both terminators in place?

    Show answer
    About 60 Ω.

  4. Why must the bus be a daisy chain and not a star?

    Show answer
    Stars and spurs create signal reflections that corrupt data.

  5. Why is a common wire recommended?

    Show answer
    It keeps the devices' signal grounds at nearly the same potential, preventing errors and transceiver damage.
