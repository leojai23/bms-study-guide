Week 3 · Day 21About 50 min✓ Done

Week 3 covered Field devices, I/O and wiring. Spend this session consolidating, not learning new material.

  1. Recap (10 min). Read the one-line summary of each page below. For any you cannot explain in your own words, re-read that page's “How it works”.
  2. Mixed practice (15 min). Answer one question from each page, out of order. Mixing topics is harder — and that is what makes it stick.
  3. Module quiz (15 min). Take the quiz and note the topics behind any wrong answers.
  4. Fix the gaps (10 min). Revisit the pages behind your wrong answers.

Recap

PageTopicIn one line
3.4Water flow metersFlow meters measure how much water passes through a pipe; add a pair of temperature sensors and a calculator and you have an energy (BTU) meter that tells you how much cooling was delivered.
3.5Damper actuatorsA damper actuator is a small motor that turns a damper's shaft to open, close or position it, in response to a control signal from the BMS.
3.6Chilled water valves and actuatorsA control valve and its actuator decide how much chilled water flows through a coil — and so how much cooling it gives. Choosing, sizing and setting them up correctly is what makes a temperature loop stable.
3.7Variable frequency drives (VFD)A VFD (variable frequency drive, also called an inverter or variable speed drive) changes a motor's speed by changing the frequency of its supply — so fans and pumps deliver only what is needed, using far less energy.
3.8Status devices: current switches and DP switchesA status device gives the BMS independent proof that equipment is really running — because a start command only shows what the BMS asked for, not what happened.
4.1Electrical safety for BMS workBMS work mixes 415 V motor circuits, 230 V panel supplies and 24 V controls in the same rooms and sometimes the same panel — so you isolate, lock, and prove dead before you touch, and you never trust a screen that says equipment is “off”.
4.2The four I/O types: DI, DO, AI, AOEvery hard-wired point is one of four types: DI and DO carry on/off states in and out of the controller; AI and AO carry a range of values in and out.
4.3Analog signals: 0–10 V, 4–20 mA, 0–20 mAAn analogue signal carries a value as a voltage (0–10 V) or a current (4–20 mA) that is scaled across a range — 12 mA on a 0–500 Pa sensor means 250 Pa.
4.4Dry contacts, relay outputs, NO and NCA contact is just a switch: NO is open and NC is closed in its normal, unoperated state; a dry contact carries no voltage of its own — the receiving side supplies it.
4.5Sensor wiring and troubleshootingGood wiring — the right cable, kept away from power, screened and terminated neatly — prevents most faults; a systematic method finds the rest quickly: check the point, measure at the controller, then split the problem in half until it is found.
4.6Reading BMS drawings and point namesBMS drawings and point names are the shared language between designer, installer, programmer and operator — read them first, keep them consistent, and update them when anything changes.

Mixed practice

  1. Convert 36 m³/h to L/s.

    from 3.4
    Show answer
    36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 L/s.

  2. Which damper type is best for modulating control, and why?

    from 3.5
    Show answer
    Opposed-blade, because its flow changes more evenly with position, giving smoother control.

  3. Why are equal-percentage valves used on coils?

    from 3.6
    Show answer
    Coils give most of their output at low flow; an equal-percentage valve compensates, so the combined response is close to linear and the loop is stable at every load.

  4. The drive ignores the BMS start command. Name two settings to check.

    from 3.7
    Show answer
    Whether the keypad is in Hand/local, and the control (command) source parameter.

  5. Where should a current switch threshold be set?

    from 3.8
    Show answer
    Between the motor's no-load current and its lowest normal running current, so a broken belt reads as “not running”.

  6. How do you measure a 4–20 mA loop current safely?

    from 4.1
    Show answer
    Put the meter in series at a designed test point or openable terminal, or use a mA clamp — never connect a meter in current mode across a supply.

  7. Classify: valve position command, CO₂ reading, fan trip, chiller enable.

    from 4.2
    Show answer
    Valve command = AO; CO₂ = AI; fan trip = DI; chiller enable = DO.

  8. Why is 4–20 mA better than 0–10 V for long cable runs?

    from 4.3
    Show answer
    Current is the same everywhere in a series loop, so cable resistance does not change the reading, and the live zero shows wire breaks.

  9. Why might a triac output fail to switch a small relay off?

    from 4.4
    Show answer
    Triacs leak a small current when off, which can be enough to keep a sensitive relay coil energised.

  10. Calculate the voltage drop for 1 A over 30 m of 1.0 mm² (0.018 Ω/m).

    from 4.5
    Show answer
    2 × 30 × 1 × 0.018 = 1.08 V.

  11. What are as-built drawings and why do they matter?

    from 4.6
    Show answer
    Drawings corrected to show what was actually installed; they are what maintenance engineers rely on for the life of the building.
