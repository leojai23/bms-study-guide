Recap, mixed practice and the module quiz.

Week 2 · Day 14About 50 min✓ Done

Week 2 covered HVAC plant + first sensors. Spend this session consolidating, not learning new material.

  1. Recap (10 min). Read the one-line summary of each page below. For any you cannot explain in your own words, re-read that page's “How it works”.
  2. Mixed practice (15 min). Answer one question from each page, out of order. Mixing topics is harder — and that is what makes it stick.
  3. Module quiz (15 min). Take the quiz and note the topics behind any wrong answers.
  4. Fix the gaps (10 min). Revisit the pages behind your wrong answers.

Recap

PageTopicIn one line
2.4VAV and CAV systemsA CAV system always delivers the same airflow and varies the supply temperature; a VAV system keeps the supply temperature roughly constant and varies the airflow to each zone.
2.5Chilled water system and chiller plantA chiller plant makes chilled water (typically about 6–7 °C) and pumps it to the AHU and FCU coils; the water returns warmer (about 12–13 °C) and goes back to the chillers to be cooled again.
2.6Cooling tower and condenser waterA cooling tower rejects the heat that water-cooled chillers take out of the building, by evaporating a small part of the condenser water into the outside air.
2.7HVAC control sequences — an overviewA sequence of operation is the written, step-by-step description of exactly how a piece of plant must start, run, stop and react to faults — the specification the BMS program is built from and tested against.
3.1Temperature sensors (NTC, PT1000)Most BMS temperature sensors are simply resistors whose value changes with temperature — an NTC's resistance falls as it warms, a PT1000's rises — and the controller converts that resistance into °C.
3.2Humidity and CO₂ sensorsHumidity sensors tell the BMS how much moisture the air holds; CO₂ sensors tell it how many people are breathing in a space — and therefore how much fresh air is needed.
3.3Differential pressure and airflow sensorsA differential pressure (ΔP) sensor measures the difference in pressure between its two ports — across a filter, between a duct and a room, or between two points in an airflow probe to work out velocity.

Mixed practice

  1. How is the AHU fan controlled in a VAV system?

    from 2.4
    Show answer
    A PID loop varies the VFD to hold duct static pressure, measured about two-thirds of the way down the duct, at its setpoint.

  2. What is low ΔT syndrome and why is it costly?

    from 2.5
    Show answer
    Chilled water returning too cold. Each chiller can only deliver part of its capacity, so more chillers and pumps run than the load needs.

  3. Define range and approach.

    from 2.6
    Show answer
    Range = CW return − CW supply. Approach = CW supply − wet-bulb temperature.

  4. Rewrite “the valve shall be controlled as required”.

    from 2.7
    Show answer
    For example: “When the supply fan is proven running, a PID loop shall modulate the CHW valve (0–100 %) to hold return air temperature at 24 °C (adjustable 22–26 °C). When the fan is off, the valve shall be closed.”

  5. How does a PT1000's resistance change with temperature?

    from 3.1
    Show answer
    It rises — about 3.85 Ω per °C, from 1,000 Ω at 0 °C.

  6. A 0–10 V, 0–2,000 ppm CO₂ sensor reads 4.5 V. What is the CO₂?

    from 3.2
    Show answer
    4.5 × 200 = 900 ppm.

  7. Why do airflow probes need a straight duct run?

    from 3.3
    Show answer
    Bends, fans and dampers make the airflow uneven and turbulent, which gives jumpy or biased readings.
