Week 1 review + Module 1 quiz

Recap, mixed practice and the module quiz.

Week 1 · Day 7About 50 min✓ Done

Week 1 covered BMS architecture + HVAC basics. Spend this session consolidating, not learning new material.

  1. Recap (10 min). Read the one-line summary of each page below. For any you cannot explain in your own words, re-read that page's “How it works”.
  2. Mixed practice (15 min). Answer one question from each page, out of order. Mixing topics is harder — and that is what makes it stick.
  3. Module quiz (15 min). Take the quiz and note the topics behind any wrong answers.
  4. Fix the gaps (10 min). Revisit the pages behind your wrong answers.

Recap

PageTopicIn one line
1.1What is a BMS?A Building Management System (BMS) is a network of controllers and software that monitors and automatically controls a building's services — mainly air-conditioning and ventilation — from one central place.
1.2BMS architecture: field, automation and management levelsA BMS is built in three layers: field devices measure and act, automation controllers run the control logic, and the management level gives people a window into the whole building.
1.3The DDC controller and how it worksA DDC (Direct Digital Control) controller is a small industrial computer that reads its inputs, runs a control program and writes its outputs — in a continuous loop, typically once a second or faster.
1.4BMS server, workstation and operator interfaceThe BMS server collects data from every controller and stores the building's history; the workstation and operator interface are how people see that data and give commands.
1.5BMS control panel architectureA BMS control panel (DDC panel) is the enclosure that houses the controller with its power supplies, protection, relays and terminals, so field wiring can be connected safely and neatly.
1.6HVAC system integrationIntegration means connecting equipment that has its own controller — chillers, VFDs, energy meters, VRF systems — to the BMS, so it can be monitored and commanded alongside everything else.
2.1Air handling unit (AHU)An AHU is a large casing that filters, cools, dehumidifies and moves air, delivering it through ducts to the spaces it serves — the single most common plant item on any BMS.
2.2Supply, return, fresh and exhaust airEvery AHU handles the same air streams: fresh air in from outside, supply air out to the rooms, return air back from the rooms and exhaust air thrown away — and the balance between them decides air quality, energy use and building pressure.
2.3Fan coil unit (FCU)An FCU is a small, local air-conditioning unit — a fan blowing room air over a chilled water coil — serving one room or zone, usually controlled by its own room thermostat or small controller.

Mixed practice

  1. Explain sense → decide → act using a room that is too warm.

    from 1.1
    Show answer
    The sensor measures 26 °C (sense). The controller compares it with the 24 °C setpoint, sees the room is too warm, and works out a bigger valve opening (decide). The actuator opens the chilled-water valve (act). The room cools, the sensor measures the new temperature, and the loop repeats.

  2. Why might a VFD be connected over a field bus as well as hard-wired?

    from 1.2
    Show answer
    The field bus gives many extra values — frequency, current, kW, energy, fault codes — for the cost of one cable. The critical start/stop and status are still hard-wired so the fan works if communication fails.

  3. Put the five steps of the scan cycle in order.

    from 1.3
    Show answer
    Read inputs → convert to engineering units → run the program → write outputs → communicate. Then repeat.

  4. Give two ways to reduce nuisance alarms.

    from 1.4
    Show answer
    Add time delays; suppress dependent alarms when plant is off by schedule; set sensible limits; review alarm counts regularly.

  5. Why should 230 V and 24 V signal wiring be kept apart?

    from 1.5
    Show answer
    For safety, and because power cables induce electrical noise into signal cables, which makes analogue readings unstable.

  6. Which AHU-01 VFD signals are hard-wired, and why?

    from 1.6
    Show answer
    Start/stop, run status, fault and speed reference — so the fan can be controlled and monitored even if the Modbus link fails.

  7. Name three BMS points belonging to a supply fan.

    from 2.1
    Show answer
    Start/stop command (DO), run status (DI), fault (DI), and speed reference (AO) if it has a VFD.

  8. Define mixed air.

    from 2.2
    Show answer
    The blend of fresh (outdoor) air and return air that enters the filters and coil.

  9. Explain the difference between 2-pipe and 4-pipe FCUs.

    from 2.3
    Show answer
    2-pipe has one coil with one supply and return pipe (cooling only, or changeover). 4-pipe has separate cooling and heating coils, each with its own pipes and valve.
