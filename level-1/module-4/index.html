Level 1 · Module 4

Digital and Analog I/O

DI, DO, AI and AO; 0–10 V and 4–20 mA; contacts and relays; wiring, testing and drawings.

This is the hands-on module. It starts with electrical safety — before you touch a terminal — then covers the four I/O types, analogue and digital signals, wiring and troubleshooting, and the drawings that tie it all together. The scaling calculator on page 4.3 and Labs 2 and 4 put it into practice.

Module 4 quiz · question 1 of 10

Which step comes immediately before testing a circuit dead?

Prove – test – prove: a tester must be shown to work before you trust a “dead” reading. (See page 4.1.)
Module 4 quiz · question 2 of 10

The BMS shows a fan OFF. Is it safe to change the belt?

Fans can be started remotely at any time. (See page 4.1.)
Module 4 quiz · question 3 of 10

A fan trip signal into the controller is a…

On/off state into the controller = digital input. (See page 4.2.)
Module 4 quiz · question 4 of 10

4–20 mA, 0–500 Pa: what current means 375 Pa?

4 + 375/500 × 16 = 4 + 12 = 16 mA. (See page 4.3.)
Module 4 quiz · question 5 of 10

A 4–20 mA input reads 1.5 mA. This indicates…

Below about 3.6 mA is treated as a fault. (See page 4.3.)
Module 4 quiz · question 6 of 10

A valve at 40 % on a 2–10 V actuator needs…

2 + 0.4 × 8 = 5.2 V. (See page 4.3.)
Module 4 quiz · question 7 of 10

“NC” on a relay contact means it is closed when…

Normal = at rest / de-energised. (See page 4.4.)
Module 4 quiz · question 8 of 10

Why is a fire shutdown loop wired closed-when-healthy?

Open circuit = fire, so faults are never hidden. (See page 4.4.)
Module 4 quiz · question 9 of 10

You measure the correct signal at the controller terminals but the BMS value is wrong. The fault is…

The field side is proven good. (See page 4.5.)
Module 4 quiz · question 10 of 10

Which document shows the final installed state?

As-builts are corrected to match what was installed. (See page 4.6.)
