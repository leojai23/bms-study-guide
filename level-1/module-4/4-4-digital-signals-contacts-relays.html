4.4 Dry contacts, relay outputs, NO and NC

Week 3 · Day 19About 10 minPage 4 of 6✓ Done

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. A 4–20 mA, 0–1,000 Pa sensor gives 8 mA. What is the pressure?

    Show answer
    (8 − 4) ÷ 16 × 1,000 = 250 Pa.
  2. What is a universal input?
    from 4.2 The four I/O types: DI, DO, AI, AO
    Show answer
    An input that can be configured in software as an AI or DI and for different signal types.
  3. Why must you isolate locally even though the BMS shows a fan as off?
    from 4.1 Electrical safety for BMS work
    Show answer
    The fan can be started at any moment by a schedule, override, another operator or another system.
In one line

A contact is just a switch: NO is open and NC is closed in its normal, unoperated state; a dry contact carries no voltage of its own — the receiving side supplies it.

?Why it matters

Most status, alarm and command signals in a BMS are contacts. Choosing NO or NC, dry or wet, and the right output type decides whether a broken wire is noticed or silently ignored — and whether a field fault destroys a controller output. These details separate a system that fails safely from one that does not.

“Normal” means de-energised

A relay's NO contact is open when its coil is off. A fire alarm relay that is held energised while the system is healthy has its “NO” contact closed in normal service. Always ask: what is the coil state when the plant is healthy?

Fail-safe: why safety signals use closed-when-healthy contacts

If a fire shutdown signal were a contact that closes on fire, a cut cable would look exactly like “no fire” — and the AHU would keep running in a real fire. Wiring it so the contact is closed when healthy and opens on fire means a broken wire also stops the AHU and raises an alarm. The same principle is used for high-pressure cut-outs, freeze protection and emergency stops.

The BMS point is configured to match: “normal = closed”. The display shows Normal when the contact is closed and Fire (or Alarm) when it opens.

Output types

OutputSwitchesNotes
RelayAC or DC, within its ratingIsolated contact; most versatile. Check the current rating and use an interposing relay for motor circuits
TriacAC only (usually 24 V AC)Small leakage current when off can keep sensitive loads (e.g. small relays) energised; cannot switch DC
Transistor / open collectorLow-voltage DC, small currentsFast — for pulse outputs and small DC relays; observe polarity
  • Suppress coils: fit an RC snubber across AC relay or contactor coils and a diode across DC coils, so switching spikes do not damage outputs or create noise.
  • Dry-circuit contacts: heavy power contacts can fail to make reliable contact at the tiny currents of a DI. Signal relays often have gold-plated contacts for this reason.
Follow AHU-01

AHU-01's fire signal comes from a relay in the fire alarm panel that is energised when healthy. Its closed contact is wired in series into the fan's starter circuit (hard-wired trip) and, through a second contact, to a BMS DI configured “normal = closed”. Fire, power loss at the fire panel interface, or a broken wire all stop the fan and show Fire shutdown. The VFD's fault relay is also wired fail-safe (closed when healthy).

⚒On site

  • Before connecting any contact to a DI, measure across it in both states — it must show 0 V (dry).
  • Use the changeover's COM/NO/NC labels on the device, not your assumption.
  • After replacing a panel or device, check every NC safety loop is reconnected — a missing link stops the plant (safely).
  • Test fail-safe loops by opening them, not just by simulating in software.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

After replacing the fire alarm interface module, AHU-01 refuses to start and the BMS shows “Fire shutdown” — but there is no fire and the fire panel shows normal. What is the most likely cause?

This is the fail-safe design doing its job: an open loop means Fire. Check the interface wiring and links — and be glad a broken wire stops the plant instead of hiding a real fire.

  1. What does “normally” mean in NO and NC?

    Show answer
    The contact's state when the device is at rest or its relay coil is de-energised — not the state during normal plant operation.

  2. Why should a fire shutdown signal be closed when healthy?

    Show answer
    So that a broken wire or loss of the signal opens the contact and stops the plant (fail-safe), instead of hiding a real fire.

  3. Can you connect a wet contact directly to a dry DI?

    Show answer
    No — use an interposing relay or an input rated for that voltage, or the input may be destroyed.

  4. Name the three terminals of a changeover contact.

    Show answer
    COM (common), NO and NC.

  5. Why might a triac output fail to switch a small relay off?

    Show answer
    Triacs leak a small current when off, which can be enough to keep a sensitive relay coil energised.
