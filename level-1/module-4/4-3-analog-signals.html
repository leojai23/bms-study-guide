Module 4 · Digital and Analog I/O

4.3 Analog signals: 0–10 V, 4–20 mA, 0–20 mA

Week 3 · Day 18About 10 minPage 3 of 6✓ Done
After this page you can
  • Explain how voltage and current signals carry a value.
  • Scale any analogue signal to engineering units — and back.
  • Wire 2-, 3- and 4-wire transmitters and check a loop's voltage budget.
  • Use the live zero of 4–20 mA to detect faults.
Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. Why add 10–20 % spare I/O?
    from 4.2 The four I/O types: DI, DO, AI, AO
    Show answer
    To allow for design changes during the project and future additions without new controllers.
  2. Why is a “24 V panel” not automatically safe?
    from 4.1 Electrical safety for BMS work
    Show answer
    It often contains 230 V supplies (transformer primary, incoming terminals) and can carry foreign voltages from other panels.
  3. Where should a current switch threshold be set?
    from 3.8 Status devices: current switches and DP switches
    Show answer
    Between the motor's no-load current and its lowest normal running current, so a broken belt reads as “not running”.
In one line

An analogue signal carries a value as a voltage (0–10 V) or a current (4–20 mA) that is scaled across a range — 12 mA on a 0–500 Pa sensor means 250 Pa.

?Why it matters

Most transmitters and almost every modulating actuator use these signals. Scaling mistakes, wrong signal types and voltage drops cause a large share of commissioning faults — and they are easy to find once you can calculate what the signal should be and measure what it is.

Scaling

Signal → value

Value = Min + (Signal − Signalmin) ÷ (Signal span) × (Max − Min)

4–20 mA, 0–500 Pa, 12 mA → 0 + (12 − 4) ÷ 16 × 500 = 250 Pa
0–10 V, 0–100 % RH, 6.2 V → 6.2 ÷ 10 × 100 = 62 % RH

Value → signal (outputs)

Signal = Signalmin + (Value ÷ 100 %) × Signal span

Valve 35 % on 0–10 V → 3.5 V. On 2–10 V → 2 + 0.35 × 8 = 4.8 V.

Wiring transmitters

TypeWiresHow it is powered
2-wire (loop-powered)2The 4–20 mA loop itself powers the transmitter — supply +, through the transmitter, through the controller input, back to supply −.
3-wire3Separate supply; supply and signal share one common (0 V) wire. Common for 0–10 V sensors.
4-wire4Separate supply pair and separate signal pair — the signal is isolated from the supply.

The 4–20 mA loop's voltage budget

A controller usually measures loop current as the voltage across an internal resistor (250 Ω turns 4–20 mA into 1–5 V). The transmitter also needs a minimum voltage across it to work. Everything must fit inside the supply voltage:

Maximum loop resistance

Rmax = (Supply − Transmitter minimum) ÷ 0.020 A

24 V supply, transmitter needs 12 V → (24 − 12) ÷ 0.020 = 600 Ω. Minus a 250 Ω input leaves 350 Ω for the cable — plenty for most buildings.

Why 0–10 V can be wrong on long cables

If a 0–10 V sensor's signal is measured against a 0 V wire that also carries actuator supply current, the voltage drop on that shared wire adds straight onto the reading. For example 0.3 A of actuator current on 50 m of 0.75 mm² cable (about 1.2 Ω) shifts the reference by about 0.37 V — 3.7 % of full scale. Use a separate signal common, keep 0–10 V runs short, or use 4–20 mA for long runs.

Using the live zero

Loop currentMeaning
Below about 3.6 mAFault: open circuit, no loop power, failed transmitter
4 mABottom of the range
20 mATop of the range
Above about 21 mAFault or over-range: short circuit, failed transmitter

These limits follow the widely used NAMUR NE 43 recommendation. Configure the AI to flag readings outside them as a sensor fault, so the program can alarm and use a fallback.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 uses both kinds. Its duct pressure sensor is a 2-wire 4–20 mA transmitter for 0–500 Pa, powered from the panel's 24 V DC supply (Lab 4). Its RH and CO₂ sensors are 3-wire 0–10 V units, and all three AOs (valve, damper, VFD speed) are 0–10 V.

⚒On site

  • Calculate the expected signal before you measure; then the measurement tells you something.
  • Check transmitter range settings (jumpers, DIP switches or menus) against the controller's scaling.
  • Measure 0–10 V between signal and the controller's own common terminal, not a nearby earth.
  • Some controller inputs need a jumper or configuration change to switch between V and mA.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

AHU-01's duct pressure (4–20 mA, 0–500 Pa) reads −31 Pa on the BMS. You measure 3.0 mA in the loop. What is the most likely cause?

3.0 mA is below the live zero, so the reading is not a real pressure. The transmitter is failing, under-powered or the loop has a high resistance. Configure the AI to flag this as a fault instead of showing −31 Pa.

  1. A 4–20 mA, 0–1,000 Pa sensor gives 8 mA. What is the pressure?

    Show answer
    (8 − 4) ÷ 16 × 1,000 = 250 Pa.

  2. What voltage drives a valve to 70 % on a 2–10 V actuator?

    Show answer
    2 + 0.7 × 8 = 7.6 V.

  3. Why is 4–20 mA better than 0–10 V for long cable runs?

    Show answer
    Current is the same everywhere in a series loop, so cable resistance does not change the reading, and the live zero shows wire breaks.

  4. A transmitter needs 10 V; the supply is 24 V. What is the maximum loop resistance?

    Show answer
    (24 − 10) ÷ 0.02 = 700 Ω.

  5. Describe how a 2-wire transmitter is connected.

    Show answer
    Supply + → transmitter + ; transmitter − → controller mA input; controller input common → supply −. The loop current powers the transmitter.
