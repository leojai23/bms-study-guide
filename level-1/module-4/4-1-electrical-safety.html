4.1 Electrical safety for BMS work

Week 3 · Day 17About 10 minPage 1 of 6✓ Done

  • Follow the safe-isolation steps, including prove–test–prove.

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. What does a fan ΔP switch prove that a VFD run relay does not?
    from 3.8 Status devices: current switches and DP switches
    Show answer
    That the fan is actually building pressure — moving air — not just that the drive output is on.
  2. What is the danger of the DC bus after isolation?
    from 3.7 Variable frequency drives (VFD)
    Show answer
    The capacitors remain charged for several minutes; touching power terminals before they discharge can be lethal.
  3. What is the advantage of a PICV?
    from 3.6 Chilled water valves and actuators
    Show answer
    Its flow depends only on its opening, not on changes in system pressure, so coils are not over- or under-supplied as other valves move; it also replaces the balancing valve.
In one line

BMS work mixes 415 V motor circuits, 230 V panel supplies and 24 V controls in the same rooms and sometimes the same panel — so you isolate, lock, and prove dead before you touch, and you never trust a screen that says equipment is “off”.

?Why it matters

Most BMS signals are low voltage, which makes it easy to forget that the panel next to them — or a terminal in the same row — can be at 230 V, and that the fan you are working on can be started by a schedule on a server in another building. This page comes before wiring on purpose. Read it, and follow your site's rules and permits: they always take precedence over this guide.

⚙How it works

Voltages you will meet

VoltageWhereNotes
415 V AC, 3-phaseMCCs, VFDs, motors, chillersLethal; arc-flash risk in large panels — authorised people only
230 V AC, single-phasePanel supplies, transformer primaries, some actuators and contactor coils, lightingLethal
24 V AC / DCControllers, actuators, sensorsExtra-low voltage (ELV) — but shares panels with 230 V
0–10 V, 4–20 mA, RS485SignalsLow energy — but can carry dangerous voltage under fault conditions
“Volt-free” is not always volt-free

Contacts that should be volt-free sometimes carry a foreign voltage — for example a 230 V control circuit from the MCC looped through BMS terminals. A DI terminal that reads 230 V will destroy the input card and can kill. Measure before you touch, and label any foreign voltage in a panel clearly.

Safe isolation

  1. Identify every source feeding what you will work on — drawings, labels, and back-feeds such as UPS supplies or MCC control circuits.
  2. Isolate at the correct isolator or MCB.
  3. Lock off and tag with your own padlock and a tag giving your name and the date. You keep the key.
  4. Prove your voltage tester on a known live source or proving unit.
  5. Test dead between every pair of conductors: L–N, L–E, N–E (and between phases on 3-phase).
  6. Re-prove the tester to show it did not fail during the test.

Using a multimeter safely

  • Use a meter and leads rated for the panel: at least CAT III 600 V for building distribution work.
  • Select the function before connecting. Never put a meter in current (A or mA) mode across a voltage — it is a short circuit through the meter.
  • To measure a 4–20 mA loop, put the meter in series at a test point or terminal designed to be opened, or use a mA clamp meter — never across the loop supply.
  • Measure resistance only on de-energised circuits, with the device disconnected.
  • Keep one hand away from the panel when probing live terminals, and use insulated probe tips.
Live work

Some diagnosis needs measurements on live circuits. That is done only when it is necessary, by a competent person, with a risk assessment and the right PPE — never as a short-cut. Your site's permit-to-work system and local electrical regulations decide who may do what.

Follow AHU-01

Before changing AHU-01's fan belt: the BMS shows the fan off by schedule — that is not isolation. Turn off and lock the AHU's local isolator (and the VFD supply), prove dead at the motor terminals, wait for the VFD's DC bus to discharge, and only then open the fan section. In panel DDC-AHU-01, remember that the incoming 230 V terminals stay live even with the controller's MCB off.

⚒On site

  • Carry your own padlock, tags and a proving unit; a tester you cannot prove is not a tester.
  • Treat every unidentified terminal as live until measured.
  • Tell the operators before isolating plant — and put the BMS point in a maintenance state so alarms do not flood.
  • After work: remove your own lock only, restore, and test the plant in Hand and Auto.

AKey terms

ELV
Extra-low voltage — not exceeding 50 V AC or 120 V DC.
Isolation
Disconnecting a circuit from all sources of supply.
Lock-out / tag-out (LOTO)
Securing an isolator with a personal lock and tag so nobody can re-energise it.
Prove – test – prove
Check the tester, test the circuit dead, check the tester again.
Foreign voltage
A voltage from another system present on terminals expected to be volt-free.
CAT rating
Measurement category of a meter, e.g. CAT III 600 V, showing where it may safely be used.
Permit to work
Formal written authorisation for a specific job under controlled conditions.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

You need to replace AHU-01's fan belt. The BMS shows the fan OFF and the schedule says it will not start until tomorrow. What must you do before opening the fan section?

Software states are not isolation: an operator override, fire mode or a schedule change can start the fan. Only a locked-off isolator, proven dead, makes it safe.

  1. Why is a “24 V panel” not automatically safe?

    Show answer
    It often contains 230 V supplies (transformer primary, incoming terminals) and can carry foreign voltages from other panels.

  2. List the six safe-isolation steps.

    Show answer
    Identify sources → isolate → lock and tag → prove tester → test dead on all conductors → re-prove tester.

  3. Why must you isolate locally even though the BMS shows a fan as off?

    Show answer
    The fan can be started at any moment by a schedule, override, another operator or another system.

  4. How do you measure a 4–20 mA loop current safely?

    Show answer
    Put the meter in series at a designed test point or openable terminal, or use a mA clamp — never connect a meter in current mode across a supply.

  5. What is a foreign voltage?

    Show answer
    A voltage from another system present on terminals that should be volt-free, e.g. a 230 V control circuit looped through BMS terminals.
