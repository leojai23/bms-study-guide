4.5 Sensor wiring and troubleshooting

Week 3 · Day 20About 10 minPage 5 of 6✓ Done
After this page you can
  • Choose cable types and apply segregation, screening and termination good practice.
  • Calculate voltage drop on 24 V supplies and signal commons.
  • Follow a systematic method to find the cause of a wrong reading.
  • Know what to measure for every signal type.
Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. Why should a fire shutdown signal be closed when healthy?
    from 4.4 Dry contacts, relay outputs, NO and NC
    Show answer
    So that a broken wire or loss of the signal opens the contact and stops the plant (fail-safe), instead of hiding a real fire.
  2. Why is 4–20 mA better than 0–10 V for long cable runs?
    from 4.3 Analog signals: 0–10 V, 4–20 mA, 0–20 mA
    Show answer
    Current is the same everywhere in a series loop, so cable resistance does not change the reading, and the live zero shows wire breaks.
  3. Give two examples of software points.
    from 4.2 The four I/O types: DI, DO, AI, AO
    Show answer
    Any two of: setpoints, occupancy mode, calculated values (e.g. fresh air fraction), alarm flags, schedules' outputs.
In one line

Good wiring — the right cable, kept away from power, screened and terminated neatly — prevents most faults; a systematic method finds the rest quickly: check the point, measure at the controller, then split the problem in half until it is found.

?Why it matters

Field wiring faults are the most common BMS faults on site, and the most time-wasting if you hunt randomly. A standard method means you can walk up to any system, from any manufacturer, and find the fault with a multimeter and the drawings.

Segregation, screens and terminations

  • Run signal cables in separate containment from power cables, or keep a clear gap — specifications often ask for around 300 mm. Cross power cables at right angles.
  • Earth cable screens at one end only, normally the panel. Earthing both ends lets current flow in the screen (an earth loop) and adds noise.
  • Keep the screen continuous through junction boxes and cut back neatly at the device.
  • Use ferrules on stranded wires, tighten to the terminal's torque, and leave no stray strands.
  • Label both ends of every cable with the number on the drawings.

Voltage drop

Two-wire supply voltage drop

Vdrop = 2 × length (m) × current (A) × resistance (Ω/m)

Copper is roughly 0.0245 Ω/m for 0.75 mm², 0.018 Ω/m for 1.0 mm² and 0.012 Ω/m for 1.5 mm².

Example: three actuators drawing 0.8 A in total, 40 m away on 0.75 mm²: 2 × 40 × 0.8 × 0.0245 ≈ 1.6 V. A 24 V AC supply arrives at about 22.4 V — usually fine, but check the actuators' minimum (often around −20 %). Long runs or many actuators need a bigger cable or a local transformer.

A method that always works

  1. Confirm the symptom against something independent — a hand-held thermometer, the device's own display, your own eyes.
  2. Check the point on the BMS: is it overridden, out of service, or configured with the wrong type, range or units?
  3. Measure at the controller terminals. If the signal there is correct but the BMS is wrong, the fault is in configuration or the controller.
  4. If the signal at the terminals is wrong, disconnect the field cable and measure the field side — cable plus device.
  5. Measure at the device. Correct at the device but wrong at the panel → cable or junction box. Wrong at the device → the device or its supply.
  6. Fix, then verify on the BMS — and write down what you found and did.

Follow AHU-01

Worked example: AHU-01's CO₂ reads 0 ppm. (1) The point is not overridden and is set for 0–10 V, 0–2,000 ppm. (2) At the controller terminals: 0.0 V. (3) Disconnected, the field cable shows 0 V from the sensor. (4) At the sensor: no 24 V supply. (5) The 24 V supply's fuse in the panel has blown — a shorted cable to a second sensor. Fix the short, replace the fuse, check the BMS shows a sensible value, and note it in the log.

⚒On site

  • Carry the drawings — the terminal numbers and cable numbers save you from guessing.
  • Change one thing at a time, and put back anything you change that does not fix the problem.
  • Photograph terminations before you disconnect anything.
  • Intermittent faults: trend the point at a short interval and wiggle-test terminations while watching.

AKey terms

Segregation
Physical separation of signal and power cables.
Screen (shield)
Conductive layer around signal pairs that intercepts electrical noise.
Earth loop
Unwanted current flowing in a screen earthed at both ends.
Ferrule
Crimped sleeve on a stranded wire end for a reliable termination.
Voltage drop
Loss of voltage along a cable due to its resistance and the current.
Half-split
Troubleshooting by testing in the middle of the chain to halve the search each time.
Wiggle test
Gently moving cables and terminals while watching a value to find intermittent faults.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

AHU-01's supply air temperature reads 3 °C higher on the BMS than a hand-held thermometer at the sensor. You disconnect the sensor and measure its resistance: it matches the hand-held temperature. What next?

The device is proven correct, so the error is between the sensor and the BMS value: a wrong NTC curve or offset in the configuration, or extra resistance in the cable or a terminal. Measure resistance at the controller end next.

  1. Why earth a cable screen at one end only?

    Show answer
    Earthing both ends lets current circulate in the screen (an earth loop), which adds noise instead of removing it.

  2. Calculate the voltage drop for 1 A over 30 m of 1.0 mm² (0.018 Ω/m).

    Show answer
    2 × 30 × 1 × 0.018 = 1.08 V.

  3. You measure the correct signal at the controller terminals but the BMS value is wrong. Where is the fault?

    Show answer
    On the controller side: point configuration (type, range, scaling, offset), an override, or the controller/input itself.

  4. How do you measure a PT1000 sensor?

    Show answer
    Power off or disconnect it, and measure its resistance; compare with the PT1000 table at the actual temperature.

  5. What is the first thing to check on the BMS when a reading is wrong?

    Show answer
    Whether the point is overridden or out of service, and that its type, range and units are configured correctly.
