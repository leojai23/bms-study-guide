2.5 Chilled water system and chiller plant

In one line

A chiller plant makes chilled water (typically about 6–7 °C) and pumps it to the AHU and FCU coils; the water returns warmer (about 12–13 °C) and goes back to the chillers to be cooled again.

?Why it matters

The chiller plant is usually the biggest energy user in the building and the most complex sequence on the BMS. Even if you never program one yourself, you need to understand it: when AHUs cannot cool, the cause is often in the plant room.

⚙How it works

What a chiller does

A chiller is a refrigeration machine. Refrigerant evaporates in the evaporator, taking heat out of the chilled water. The compressor raises the refrigerant's pressure and temperature. In the condenser the refrigerant gives that heat away — to outdoor air (air-cooled chillers) or to condenser water and a cooling tower (water-cooled chillers, page 2.6). An expansion valve drops the pressure again and the cycle repeats. The chiller's own controller runs all of this; the BMS enables it, watches it and may reset its setpoint.

Cooling capacity

Cooling (kW) = 4.19 × flow (L/s) × ΔT (K)  ·  1 TR (ton of refrigeration) = 3.517 kW

Example: 20 L/s through a coil, 7 °C in and 12.5 °C out → 4.19 × 20 × 5.5 ≈ 461 kW ≈ 131 TR.

Pumping arrangements

ArrangementHow it worksNotes
Constant primaryFixed-speed pumps push constant flow through chillers and 3-way valves at the coilsOlder, simple; wastes pump energy
Primary-secondaryConstant-flow primary pumps serve the chillers; variable-speed secondary pumps serve the building; a decoupler pipe joins the twoVery common; robust
Variable primary flow (VPF)One set of variable-speed pumps through chillers and building; a bypass valve keeps minimum chiller flowSaves pump energy; needs careful control

With 2-way valves at the coils, building flow falls as valves close. The secondary (or VPF) pumps are slowed to hold a differential pressure measured across the far end of the system (the index circuit), so even the most remote coil always has enough pressure.

The chiller start sequence

  1. Cooling is needed (schedule, outdoor temperature or demand from AHUs).
  2. Open the chiller's isolation valves (if motorised) and start its chilled water pump.
  3. Prove chilled water flow (flow switch or the chiller's own flow sensing).
  4. For a water-cooled chiller: start the condenser water pump and the cooling tower; prove condenser flow.
  5. Enable the chiller. Its own controller starts the compressor and loads up to hold its leaving water setpoint.
  6. Stopping is the reverse: disable the chiller, then keep the pumps running for a few minutes (run-on) before stopping them.
Never without flow

A chiller must never run without proven water flow through its evaporator and condenser — the water can freeze in the evaporator or the pressure can trip the machine. Flow proving is an interlock, not an option.

Staging: how many chillers?

  • Stage up (start another chiller) when the running chillers are near full load for a sustained time, or supply water stays above setpoint — e.g. above 90 % load for 15 minutes, or CHW supply more than 1 K above setpoint.
  • Stage down when the remaining chillers could carry the load with margin — e.g. total load would be under about 70–80 % of the remaining capacity for 15 minutes.
  • Use time delays and minimum run and off times so chillers do not short-cycle.
  • Rotate the lead chiller by run-hours so wear is shared.

Low ΔT syndrome

Chillers are designed for a certain temperature difference — say 5.5 K. If water comes back from the building too cold (a small ΔT), each chiller can only deliver part of its capacity, so more chillers and pumps run for the same load. Causes include 3-way valves, valves stuck open or passing, coils fouled, setpoints set too low, and too much flow forced through coils. Trending CHW return temperature is one of the best checks of plant health.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01's coil is one of the loads on the secondary loop. Its 2-way valve takes as much 7 °C water as it needs. As the floor cools and the valve closes, the index ΔP rises and the secondary pumps slow down. Its design is 7 °C in and about 12.5 °C out — if it returns colder than that, AHU-01 is contributing to low ΔT.

⚒On site

  • Treat the chiller's own controller as the authority for the machine. The BMS enables it and may reset its setpoint — it does not run the compressor.
  • Check strainers and isolation valves whenever flow is low or a chiller trips on flow.
  • Record the CHW supply and return temperatures every visit; a shrinking ΔT is an early warning.
  • Know where the index ΔP sensor is and what its setpoint is — it decides every pump's speed.

AKey terms

Chiller
Refrigeration machine that produces chilled water.
Evaporator / condenser
Heat exchangers where the refrigerant takes heat from chilled water and gives it to air or condenser water.
Primary-secondary
Pumping arrangement with constant chiller flow and variable building flow, joined by a decoupler.
Variable primary flow
Single set of variable-speed pumps serving chillers and building.
Decoupler
Short pipe joining the primary and secondary loops so their flows can differ.
Index circuit
The hydraulically furthest circuit, where the ΔP sensor is placed.
Staging
Starting or stopping chillers to match the load.
TR
Ton of refrigeration = 3.517 kW of cooling.
Low ΔT syndrome
Chilled water returning too cold, so chillers cannot load fully.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

Three chillers are running, each at about 40 % load. CHW supply is 7 °C but the return is only 9.5 °C (design 12.5 °C). What is the plant suffering from?

With a ΔT of 2.5 K instead of 5.5 K, each chiller can deliver less than half its capacity, so extra chillers and pumps run. Look for passing or stuck-open coil valves, 3-way valves and low supply air setpoints.

  1. Name the four main parts of the refrigeration cycle in a chiller.

    Show answer
    Evaporator, compressor, condenser, expansion valve.

  2. Calculate the cooling for 15 L/s with a 5 K temperature difference.

    Show answer
    4.19 × 15 × 5 ≈ 314 kW ≈ 89 TR.

  3. What does the decoupler do in a primary-secondary plant?

    Show answer
    Joins the primary and secondary loops so the chiller flow (constant) and building flow (variable) can differ; the difference flows through the decoupler.

  4. List the start sequence for a water-cooled chiller.

    Show answer
    Start CHW pump → prove CHW flow → start CW pump and tower → prove CW flow → enable chiller.

  5. What is low ΔT syndrome and why is it costly?

    Show answer
    Chilled water returning too cold. Each chiller can only deliver part of its capacity, so more chillers and pumps run than the load needs.
