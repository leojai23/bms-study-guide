2.1 Air handling unit (AHU)

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)

  2. List the signals usually exchanged between the BMS and a fan's MCC starter.
    from 1.5 BMS control panel architecture

  3. Why are forgotten overrides a problem?

In one line

An AHU is a large casing that filters, cools, dehumidifies and moves air, delivering it through ducts to the spaces it serves — the single most common plant item on any BMS.

?Why it matters

Nearly every BMS engineer's first point list, first wiring diagram and first sequence is for an AHU, and most comfort complaints in a commercial building lead back to one. If you can read an AHU — what each section does, which points it needs and how it is controlled — you can handle most of the HVAC side of a BMS.

⚙How it works

The sections, in airflow order

SectionWhat it doesTypical BMS points

Cooling coilCools the air with chilled water and removes moistureCHW valve AO
Drain panCollects water condensed on the coilCondensate overflow DI (optional)

Supply fanMoves the air through the ductsStart DO, run status DI, fault DI, speed AO
Discharge / silencerReduces noise; air leaves for the ductsSupply air temperature AI

Bigger systems add a return fan and an exhaust (relief) damper, a humidifier for dry climates or special areas, or a heat-recovery wheel that pre-cools incoming fresh air with outgoing exhaust air.

How the coil removes moisture

Air can hold only so much water vapour at a given temperature. When the cooling coil chills the air below its dew point, some of the vapour condenses on the coil fins as water, drips into the drain pan and leaves through the condensate drain. That is how an AHU dehumidifies — and why the air leaving a chilled-water coil is usually only about 12–14 °C. In humid climates removing moisture is often as important as lowering temperature.

What does the valve control to?

The CHW valve is always modulated to hold some temperature. Which one is a design decision:

Control on…How it worksGood forWatch out for
Return air temperatureReturn air is taken as the average room temperatureSimple single-zone AHUs; very commonReturn air is affected by ceiling heat and leaky ducts
Supply air temperatureHolds the air leaving the coil at e.g. 13 °CVAV systems; TFAsRooms are not controlled directly; usually reset from zone demand
Space temperatureA room sensor drives the valveSingle rooms or hallsOne sensor must represent the whole zone
Cascade (space → supply)Room loop sets the supply air setpoint; supply loop drives the valveBest comfort and stabilityTwo loops to tune
Typical values (always check the design)
  • Supply air leaving a CHW coil: about 12–15 °C.
  • Return air / room: about 23–25 °C.
  • Chilled water: about 6–7 °C in, 12–13 °C out.
  • Filter pressure drop: low when clean, rising as dust builds up; the dirty-filter alarm is set from the filter maker's recommended final pressure drop.
Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 is a recirculating CAV-style AHU: modulating fresh air damper, manual return damper, one filter stage with a ΔP switch, a CHW coil with a 2-way valve, and a supply fan on a VFD. It controls on return air temperature (24 °C) and monitors supply air temperature for alarms.

⚒On site

  • Check the airflow-direction arrows on filters, coils and dampers — parts are sometimes fitted backwards.
  • Open the access door and look: is the drain pan clean, and is the condensate trap filled with water? A dry trap lets air suck the condensate back and the pan overflows.
  • Find out whether the valve is 2-way or 3-way and whether it is a PICV — it changes how the plant behaves (page 3.6).
  • Note the fan type: belt drive (belts slip and break) or direct drive / EC fan.
  • Read the AHU's nameplate and the commissioning sheet: design airflow, external static pressure, coil capacity.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

The 3rd-floor offices are hot. The AHU-01 graphic shows the CHW valve at 100 % and supply air at 18 °C (normally 13 °C). The fan is running at its usual speed. Where do you look first?

The controller is already asking for full cooling (valve 100 %), yet the coil cannot cool the air. The problem is the water side: high CHW supply temperature, a closed isolation valve, a blocked strainer or a plant fault. Tuning cannot help when the output is already at its limit.

  1. List the sections of a typical AHU in airflow order.

    Show answer
    Dampers and mixing box → pre-filter → fine filter → cooling coil (with drain pan) → heating coil if fitted → supply fan → discharge/silencer.

  2. How does an AHU remove moisture from the air?

    Show answer
    The cooling coil chills the air below its dew point, so water vapour condenses on the coil, collects in the drain pan and is drained away.

  3. What is a TFA and where is it used?

    Show answer
    A treated fresh air unit: an AHU handling 100 % outdoor air, cooling and dehumidifying it before supplying it to floors or FCU zones — common in hotels, offices with FCUs and hospitals.

  4. Name three BMS points belonging to a supply fan.

    Show answer
    Start/stop command (DO), run status (DI), fault (DI), and speed reference (AO) if it has a VFD.

  5. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of return air temperature control.

    Show answer
    Advantage: simple — the return air represents the average room temperature. Disadvantage: it can be distorted by ceiling-void heat and duct leakage, and it reacts slowly.
