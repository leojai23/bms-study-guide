2.2 Supply, return, fresh and exhaust air

    Show answer

  2. Why must you be careful when testing writes on live plant?
    from 1.6 HVAC system integration
    Show answer
    Writing a setpoint or parameter changes how real equipment runs; a mistake can trip plant or affect occupants.
  3. Why are field cables landed on terminal blocks rather than directly on the controller?
    from 1.5 BMS control panel architecture
    Show answer
    Terminal blocks give a clear, numbered connection point for testing and disconnection, protect the controller's terminals from repeated work, and let the controller be replaced without disturbing field wiring.
In one line

Every AHU handles the same air streams: fresh air in from outside, supply air out to the rooms, return air back from the rooms and exhaust air thrown away — and the balance between them decides air quality, energy use and building pressure.

?Why it matters

Fresh air is essential for the people in the building, but in a hot, humid climate it is also the biggest single cooling load. Get the balance wrong and you either waste energy or leave people in stuffy rooms. Many BMS points — damper positions, CO₂ readings, mixed air temperature — exist only to manage this balance.

Why fresh air — and why it costs

People breathe out CO₂ and moisture; materials give off odours and chemicals. Fresh air dilutes them. Ventilation standards — ASHRAE 62.1 internationally and the National Building Code in India — set minimum fresh air rates per person and per floor area. For a typical office this works out at very roughly 8–10 litres per second per person.

On a 35 °C humid afternoon every litre of that air must be cooled and dried to about 13 °C. That is why buildings do not simply use 100 % fresh air everywhere, and why controlling fresh air to the actual occupancy (CO₂ control, page 3.2 and Module 6) saves so much energy.

Mixed air temperature — a calculation worth knowing

Mixed air temperature is the weighted average of the two streams:

Formula

MAT = (fresh fraction × fresh air temp) + (return fraction × return air temp)

Example: 20 % fresh air at 35 °C with 80 % return air at 24 °C → MAT = 0.2 × 35 + 0.8 × 24 = 7 + 19.2 = 26.2 °C.

Turn it round and you can measure the fresh air fraction from three temperature sensors — a quick site check that the fresh air damper is really doing what the BMS says:

Fresh air fraction

Fresh fraction = (MAT − RAT) ÷ (OAT − RAT)

Example: MAT 26.2 °C, RAT 24 °C, OAT 35 °C → (2.2) ÷ (11) = 0.20, i.e. 20 %.

It only works when outdoor and return temperatures are several degrees apart.

How much cooling does the air carry?

For air, a useful approximation of the sensible cooling delivered is:

Sensible heat

Cooling (W) ≈ 1.2 × airflow (L/s) × ΔT (K)

Example: 1,000 L/s supplied at 13 °C into a room at 24 °C → 1.2 × 1,000 × 11 ≈ 13 kW.

Building pressure

A building is usually kept slightly positive — a little more air supplied than exhausted — so that dust, heat and humid air do not leak in through doors and gaps. Toilets, kitchens and car parks are kept negative so their smells stay put. Symptoms of the wrong balance: doors that whistle, slam or are hard to open, and humid air drifting in at entrances.

Free cooling (economiser)

In mild climates, when outdoor air is cooler and drier than return air, an AHU can open its fresh air damper fully and cool with outside air for free. In hot, humid climates the opportunity is small — outdoor air is rarely both cooler and drier — so economiser logic must compare enthalpy (heat and moisture), not just temperature.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01's fresh air damper has a minimum position of 20 % for the design occupancy. The return air CO₂ sensor lets the BMS open it further when the floor is busy (Module 6). The return damper is set manually during balancing. Toilet exhaust fans keep the toilets negative; the offices stay slightly positive.

⚒On site

  • Watch the damper linkages actually move when you command them — a slipped shaft clamp can leave a damper stuck while the actuator turns.
  • Use the fresh air fraction formula with a hand thermometer to check what the dampers are really doing.
  • Feel for air movement at entrance doors: strong inward draught means the building is negative.
  • If an outdoor air sensor reads high every afternoon, look at where the sun reaches it.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

On a hot afternoon AHU-02 shows OAT 34 °C, RAT 24 °C and MAT 24.3 °C. The BMS says the fresh air damper is at 30 %. What does this tell you?

Fresh fraction = (24.3 − 24) ÷ (34 − 24) = 0.03. If the damper were really at 30 %, MAT would be about 27 °C. Check the damper on site: actuator, shaft clamp, linkage — or a blocked intake.

  1. Define mixed air.

    Show answer
    The blend of fresh (outdoor) air and return air that enters the filters and coil.

  2. Calculate MAT for 25 % fresh air at 36 °C and 75 % return air at 24 °C.

    Show answer
    0.25 × 36 + 0.75 × 24 = 9 + 18 = 27 °C.

  3. Why is fresh air the largest cooling load in a hot, humid climate?

    Show answer
    Every litre must be cooled from outdoor temperature (e.g. 35 °C) and dried — removing moisture takes a lot of energy — whereas return air is already near room conditions.

  4. Why are buildings kept slightly positive?

    Show answer
    So that unconditioned, dusty or humid outdoor air does not leak in through doors and gaps.

  5. Where should an outdoor air temperature sensor be mounted?

    Show answer
    In the shade (north-facing), in a ventilated radiation shield, away from exhausts, condensing units and hot roof surfaces.
