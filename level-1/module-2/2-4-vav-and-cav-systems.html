2.4 VAV and CAV systems

Week 2 · Day 8About 10 minPage 4 of 7✓ Done

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. What does an FCU not provide that an AHU does?
    from 2.3 Fan coil unit (FCU)
    Show answer
    Fresh air — an FCU only recirculates room air. Fresh air comes from a separate TFA.
  2. Calculate MAT for 25 % fresh air at 36 °C and 75 % return air at 24 °C.
    from 2.2 Supply, return, fresh and exhaust air
    Show answer
    0.25 × 36 + 0.75 × 24 = 9 + 18 = 27 °C.
  3. What is a TFA and where is it used?
    from 2.1 Air handling unit (AHU)
    Show answer
    A treated fresh air unit: an AHU handling 100 % outdoor air, cooling and dehumidifying it before supplying it to floors or FCU zones — common in hotels, offices with FCUs and hospitals.
In one line

A CAV system always delivers the same airflow and varies the supply temperature; a VAV system keeps the supply temperature roughly constant and varies the airflow to each zone.

?Why it matters

Most modern multi-zone office AHUs are VAV. They save a lot of fan energy, but they add a second layer of control — a box and controller in every zone, and a fan speed loop on the AHU — that must be set up correctly. Many comfort and noise complaints in VAV buildings are really control problems.

Inside a VAV box

A VAV box (terminal unit) sits in the duct above the ceiling. It has an inlet flow sensor (usually a cross-shaped tube measuring velocity pressure), a damper with an actuator, a small controller, and sometimes a reheat coil. Most modern boxes are pressure-independent: they control airflow, not damper position, so a pressure change in the duct does not upset the room.

  1. The zone sensor reads 25.1 °C against a 24 °C setpoint.
  2. The temperature loop calculates the airflow needed — somewhere between the box's minimum (for ventilation) and maximum (design) airflow.
  3. The flow loop moves the damper until the measured airflow matches that setpoint.
  4. If duct pressure changes, the flow loop corrects the damper straight away — the room never notices.

Controlling the AHU fan

As boxes close, pressure in the supply duct rises. A duct static pressure sensor, usually about two-thirds of the way along the longest duct run, measures it. A PID loop varies the fan's VFD to hold a setpoint chosen during commissioning (often a few hundred pascals). The supply air temperature is held at about 13 °C by the coil valve, as in any AHU.

Static pressure reset

A fixed pressure setpoint is usually higher than needed most of the time. With trim and respond reset, the BMS lowers the setpoint a little at a time until one box damper is nearly fully open, and raises it when boxes report they are starved. Same comfort, less fan energy and less noise. ASHRAE Guideline 36 describes standard sequences for this.

Why VAV saves so much energy: the fan laws

When fan speed changes by…Airflow changes by…Pressure changes by…Power changes by…
100 % → 80 %80 %64 % (0.8²)≈ 51 % (0.8³)
100 % → 60 %60 %36 %≈ 22 %
100 % → 50 %50 %25 %≈ 13 %

Power falls with the cube of speed: running at 80 % speed needs only about half the power. Real systems save a little less than the ideal law suggests, but the savings are still large.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 is run as a constant-volume AHU: its VFD is set to a fixed commissioned speed (42 Hz) and its valve controls return air temperature. Its duct static pressure sensor is used for monitoring and a high-pressure alarm. If VAV boxes were added to the floor, the VFD would be switched to control that pressure — the sensor is already in place.

⚒On site

  • Check every VAV box's minimum and maximum airflow against the design schedule.
  • The flow sensor's two tubes (high and low) must not be swapped, kinked or blocked — the controller will read nonsense.
  • Look at the duct static pressure sensor's tubing as well as its reading: a loose tube is a common cause of runaway fans.
  • Check damper rotation: does “open” on the BMS really open the damper?
  • Trend fan speed and the most-open box damper together — they show whether the pressure setpoint is right.

AKey terms

CAV
Constant air volume — fixed airflow, varying supply temperature.
VAV
Variable air volume — varying airflow to each zone at roughly constant supply temperature.
VAV box
Terminal unit with a damper, flow sensor and controller serving one zone.
Pressure-independent
A box that controls airflow, so duct pressure changes do not affect the room.
Duct static pressure
Pressure inside the supply duct relative to the surroundings; used to control the fan.
Trim and respond
Reset method that lowers a setpoint until a zone asks for more.
Fan affinity laws
Flow ∝ speed, pressure ∝ speed², power ∝ speed³.
Minimum airflow
The lowest airflow a box may supply, for ventilation and air distribution.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

After a filter change on AHU-02 (a VAV AHU), its VFD runs at 50 Hz, diffusers whistle and duct static pressure on the BMS reads 0 Pa. What has most likely happened?

The loop sees 0 Pa, far below setpoint, so it ramps the fan to full speed — while real duct pressure is very high. Reconnect the tube. A good program also alarms on “fan at maximum with pressure near zero”.

  1. What varies in a CAV system and what varies in a VAV system?

    Show answer
    CAV: airflow is fixed; supply temperature varies. VAV: supply temperature is roughly constant; airflow to each zone varies.

  2. What does “pressure-independent” mean for a VAV box?

    Show answer
    The box controls measured airflow, not just damper position, so it compensates automatically for changes in duct pressure.

  3. How is the AHU fan controlled in a VAV system?

    Show answer
    A PID loop varies the VFD to hold duct static pressure, measured about two-thirds of the way down the duct, at its setpoint.

  4. A fan slows from 100 % to 70 % speed. Roughly what power does it need?

    Show answer
    0.7³ ≈ 0.34, so about a third of full-speed power.

  5. Why does a VAV box have a minimum airflow?

    Show answer
    To keep supplying enough fresh air for ventilation and enough air movement for good distribution, even when the zone needs little cooling.
