2.6 Cooling tower and condenser water

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)

    Show answer
    Joins the primary and secondary loops so the chiller flow (constant) and building flow (variable) can differ; the difference flows through the decoupler.
  2. A fan slows from 100 % to 70 % speed. Roughly what power does it need?
    from 2.4 VAV and CAV systems

  3. A whole floor of FCUs goes offline at once. What kind of fault does that suggest?
    from 2.3 Fan coil unit (FCU)
    Show answer
    A shared fault: the field-bus wiring, termination, power, or the supervisory controller — not the individual units.
In one line

A cooling tower rejects the heat that water-cooled chillers take out of the building, by evaporating a small part of the condenser water into the outside air.

?Why it matters

Heat removed from every room has to go somewhere. In water-cooled plants it leaves through the cooling tower. If the tower cannot reject heat — fans off, fill clogged, a very humid day — the chillers lose capacity and eventually trip. Tower control also has a direct effect on chiller efficiency.

⚙How it works

How it works

  1. Warm condenser water (about 37 °C) from the chiller is sprayed over the fill — a large surface of thin plastic sheets.
  2. The fan draws outdoor air up through the fill.
  3. A small part of the water evaporates. Evaporation takes a lot of heat, so the rest of the water cools.
  4. Cooled water (about 32 °C) collects in the basin and the condenser water pump sends it back to the chiller.

Range, approach and wet-bulb

TermMeaningTypical
Wet-bulb temperatureThe lowest temperature water can be cooled to by evaporation; depends on humidity. It is the tower's limit — not the ordinary (dry-bulb) temperature.26–29 °C on humid Indian summer days
RangeWater in minus water out (CW return − CW supply)About 5 K
ApproachWater out minus wet-bulb (CW supply − wet-bulb)About 3–5 K
You cannot beat the wet-bulb

Condenser water leaving the tower can never be colder than the wet-bulb temperature, and in practice not closer than the approach. A setpoint of 26 °C on a day with a 28 °C wet-bulb will simply run every fan at full speed for nothing.

Controlling the fans

The BMS holds the condenser water supply temperature (water leaving the tower) at a setpoint by varying fan speed (VFDs) or by switching fans or cells on and off. Colder condenser water makes the chiller more efficient but costs fan energy; and each chiller has a minimum condenser water temperature it needs to run properly. The best setpoint often floats with the wet-bulb (e.g. wet-bulb + approach).

The heat rejected is more than the cooling load — the tower also gets rid of the compressor's energy — typically about 1.2–1.3 times the cooling delivered.

Water and water treatment

Evaporation leaves minerals behind, so the water gets steadily more concentrated. A conductivity sensor opens a blowdown valve to drain some concentrated water, and a float or level control adds make-up water. Chemical dosing controls scale, corrosion and bacteria — including Legionella, which is why towers need regular cleaning and treatment records.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 has no direct link to the tower, but the heat it removes from the 3rd floor ends up there. On a very humid afternoon, if the towers struggle, the chillers lose capacity, CHW supply temperature rises — and AHU-01's valve goes to 100 % without bringing the floor down. Plant-room problems show up as office complaints.

⚒On site

  • Check fan rotation and the vibration switch after any motor or belt work.
  • Look at the basin level and the make-up valve; an overflowing basin wastes water and chemicals.
  • Compare CW supply temperature with the current wet-bulb before calling the tower faulty.
  • Make sure isolation valves on unused cells are closed, or water bypasses the working fill.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

The outdoor wet-bulb is 28 °C. An operator has set the condenser water supply setpoint to 26 °C “to save chiller energy”. What happens?

Water leaving the tower cannot be colder than the wet-bulb (28 °C) and realistically stays a few degrees above it. Set the setpoint at wet-bulb + approach, or let it float, respecting the chiller's minimum.

  1. How does a cooling tower cool water?

    Show answer
    By evaporation: a small part of the water evaporates into air drawn through the fill, taking heat with it and cooling the rest.

  2. Define range and approach.

    Show answer
    Range = CW return − CW supply. Approach = CW supply − wet-bulb temperature.

  3. Why is the wet-bulb temperature, not the dry-bulb, the tower's limit?

    Show answer
    Evaporation can only cool water down to the wet-bulb temperature, which depends on the air's humidity.

  4. What is blowdown and why is it needed?

    Show answer
    Draining part of the tower water so that minerals concentrated by evaporation do not build up into scale and corrosion.

  5. Name four cooling tower points.

    Show answer
    Any four of: fan start/stop, run status, fault, speed, vibration switch, CW supply and return temperature, basin level, make-up water meter, conductivity, blowdown valve.
