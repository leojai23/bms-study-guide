3.1 Temperature sensors (NTC, PT1000)

In one line

Most BMS temperature sensors are simply resistors whose value changes with temperature — an NTC's resistance falls as it warms, a PT1000's rises — and the controller converts that resistance into °C.

?Why it matters

Temperature is the most measured value in any BMS. Every control loop that holds a room, a duct or a pipe at a setpoint relies on a sensor being the right type, in the right place, wired correctly and configured to match. Getting any one of those wrong gives a reading that looks plausible but is wrong — the worst kind of fault.

⚙How it works

Match the curve

“10k NTC” is not one sensor. There are several 10k curves (often described by a β value such as 3435 or 3950, or by maker-specific “types”). If the controller input is set to a different curve from the sensor fitted, the reading is right at 25 °C and increasingly wrong away from it. Always check the sensor datasheet against the input configuration.

Accuracy

PT1000 accuracy is set by class in IEC 60751: Class B is ±(0.3 + 0.005 × |t|) °C — about ±0.3 °C at 0 °C — and Class A is ±(0.15 + 0.002 × |t|) °C. NTC sensors for HVAC are typically ±0.2 to ±0.5 °C around room temperature. In practice, location errors (sun, draughts, a sensor touching the duct wall) are usually bigger than the sensor's own error.

Wiring resistance

Most BMS resistive sensors use two wires. The controller measures the sensor plus both wires, so cable resistance adds to the reading:

50 m of 0.75 mm² cable (≈ 2.5 Ω loop)Error
10k NTC (≈ 440 Ω/°C)≈ 0.006 °C — negligible
PT1000 (3.85 Ω/°C)≈ 0.6 °C — usually acceptable, or compensate with an offset
PT100 (0.385 Ω/°C)≈ 6.5 °C — unacceptable with 2 wires; needs 3- or 4-wire connection

This is why BMS uses PT1000 rather than PT100, and why many controllers let you enter an offset or the cable resistance.

What the reading tells you when it fails

FaultNTC reads…PT1000 reads…
Open circuit (broken wire, loose terminal)Very low (resistance → ∞)Very high
Short circuit (wires touching)Very high (resistance → 0)Very low

Most controllers show these as “out of range” or a fault flag. A good program also alarms on an impossible value and uses a fallback.

Transmitter versions

Where the cable is long or noisy, use a temperature transmitter that converts the reading into 4–20 mA or 0–10 V (Module 4). The controller then reads a scaled signal instead of a resistance.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 has two temperature sensors: a return air duct probe (RAT) and a supply air duct probe (SAT), both 10k NTC on universal inputs configured to the same curve as the sensors. RAT drives the valve loop; SAT is used for monitoring and the low-supply-temperature alarm.

⚒On site

  • Check the sensor's datasheet curve against the input configuration before blaming the sensor.
  • To test a sensor, disconnect it and measure its resistance with a multimeter; compare with the table at a known temperature.
  • Compare with a calibrated hand-held thermometer placed right next to the sensing tip.
  • Fill thermowells with thermal paste — an empty well adds lag and error.
  • Label sensor cables at both ends; swapped RAT and SAT sensors are a classic commissioning mistake.

AKey terms

NTC thermistor
Sensor whose resistance falls as temperature rises; 10k at 25 °C is common in HVAC.
RTD
Resistance temperature detector — a metal (usually platinum) sensor whose resistance rises with temperature.
PT1000
Platinum RTD with 1,000 Ω at 0 °C, about 3.85 Ω per °C (IEC 60751).
β (beta) value
Number describing the shape of an NTC's curve, e.g. 3435 or 3950.
Thermowell
Closed tube in a pipe into which an immersion sensor is inserted.
Averaging sensor
Long capillary sensor giving the average temperature across a duct.
Lead resistance
Resistance of the sensor cable, which adds to the measured resistance.
Accuracy class
IEC 60751 tolerance band for PT sensors, e.g. Class A or B.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

After ceiling works, AHU-01's supply air temperature suddenly reads −40 °C. The AHU is running and the offices are comfortable. What is the most likely cause?

An NTC reads very low when its circuit is open (infinite resistance). Comfortable offices confirm the real air is fine. Measure resistance at the controller terminals with the sensor disconnected.

  1. How does a PT1000's resistance change with temperature?

    Show answer
    It rises — about 3.85 Ω per °C, from 1,000 Ω at 0 °C.

  2. Why can't you swap any 10k NTC for another?

    Show answer
    Different 10k NTCs follow different curves (β values or maker types). The controller input must be set to the same curve as the sensor, otherwise readings drift away from 25 °C.

  3. An NTC input reads a very high temperature. Open or short circuit?

    Show answer
    Short circuit — the resistance has dropped to near zero, which an NTC reports as very hot.

  4. Why is PT1000 preferred over PT100 for 2-wire BMS inputs?

    Show answer
    Its sensitivity is ten times higher (3.85 vs 0.385 Ω/°C), so cable resistance causes a ten times smaller error.

  5. Where should the tip of an immersion sensor sit?

    Show answer
    In the middle third of the pipe, in a thermowell filled with thermal paste.
