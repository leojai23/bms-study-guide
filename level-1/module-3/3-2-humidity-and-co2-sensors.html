3.2 Humidity and CO₂ sensors

  3. Why is the wet-bulb temperature, not the dry-bulb, the tower's limit?
    from 2.6 Cooling tower and condenser water
    Show answer

In one line

Humidity sensors tell the BMS how much moisture the air holds; CO₂ sensors tell it how many people are breathing in a space — and therefore how much fresh air is needed.

?Why it matters

In humid climates, moisture control matters as much as temperature: too humid and rooms feel clammy and mould grows. CO₂ is the cheapest reliable way to measure occupancy, and using it to control fresh air is one of the biggest energy savings a BMS can deliver. Both sensors drift over time, so you need to know how to check them.

⚙How it works

Humidity in one minute

TermMeaningExample
Relative humidity (RH)How full of moisture the air is, compared with the most it could hold at that temperature (%)50 % RH at 24 °C
Dew pointThe temperature at which that air would start to condense24 °C, 50 % RH → dew point ≈ 13 °C
Absolute humidityActual mass of water per kg of dry air (g/kg)≈ 9.3 g/kg for 24 °C, 50 % RH

RH depends on temperature: cool the same air and its RH rises, even though the moisture is unchanged. That is why RH is always read together with temperature — most humidity sensors are combined temperature + RH units. Most offices aim for roughly 40–60 % RH; above about 60 % comfort falls and mould risk rises.

How humidity sensors work

Most BMS humidity sensors are capacitive: a thin polymer layer absorbs moisture, changing the capacitance of a tiny capacitor. Electronics convert this into a 0–10 V or 4–20 mA signal for 0–100 % RH (some sensors communicate over Modbus or BACnet instead).

  • Typical accuracy: about ±2–3 % RH in the middle of the range.
  • Sensors drift slowly and can be damaged by condensation, dust and chemicals. Check them against a calibrated hand-held instrument every year or two.
  • Never mount them where they can get wet — e.g. right after a cooling coil, where air is close to saturation.

CO₂ — the occupancy sensor

People breathe out CO₂. Outdoor air is around 420 ppm (parts per million). In an occupied room, CO₂ rises until the fresh air supplied balances what people breathe out. Many designs aim to keep indoor CO₂ below about 1,000 ppm — roughly 600 ppm above outdoor air.

BMS CO₂ sensors use NDIR (non-dispersive infrared): CO₂ absorbs infrared light at a particular wavelength, so the sensor measures how much light is absorbed across a small chamber. Output is commonly 0–10 V for 0–2,000 ppm — so 1 V = 200 ppm.

Automatic calibration has a catch

Many single-channel CO₂ sensors use automatic baseline calibration (ABC): they assume the lowest reading over the last week or so is outdoor air (≈ 400 ppm) and correct themselves to it. That only works if the space really empties overnight or at weekends. In 24/7 spaces — hospitals, control rooms, some hotels — ABC drags the readings too low. Use dual-channel sensors there, or turn ABC off and calibrate manually.

Demand-controlled ventilation (DCV)

Instead of supplying fresh air for the design number of people all day, DCV opens the fresh air damper only as far as the measured CO₂ requires. Empty meeting rooms and half-full offices then cost far less to cool. The minimum position still covers the building's own emissions (materials, furniture).

Do not mix up CO and CO₂

CO₂ (carbon dioxide) measures people. CO (carbon monoxide) is a toxic gas from engines and burners. They use different sensors and different limits.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 has a combined return air humidity sensor (0–10 V = 0–100 % RH) and a return air CO₂ sensor (0–10 V = 0–2,000 ppm). The CO₂ value drives the fresh air damper between 20 % at 700 ppm and 50 % at 1,000 ppm, exactly as in the graph. The humidity value is trended and alarmed above 65 % RH.

⚒On site

  • Check scaling: a CO₂ sensor reading exactly half or double the expected value usually has the wrong range configured (e.g. 0–2,000 vs 0–1,000 ppm).
  • Compare an RH sensor with a calibrated hand-held hygrometer at the same spot and temperature.
  • Room CO₂ sensors belong at breathing height, away from doors, windows and supply diffusers.
  • Ask whether a space is ever empty before relying on a CO₂ sensor's automatic calibration.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

A 0–10 V CO₂ sensor shows 6 V. The BMS is set for 0–2,000 ppm. What does the BMS display, and what does that mean for AHU-01's fresh air damper?

6 V × 200 ppm/V = 1,200 ppm. That is above the 1,000 ppm point, so DCV drives the fresh air damper to its 50 % maximum.

  1. Why is RH always measured together with temperature?

    Show answer
    RH depends on temperature: the same air shows a higher RH when cooler. Without temperature you cannot interpret it or calculate dew point.

  2. What does a CO₂ sensor tell a BMS?

    Show answer
    How much fresh air is needed for the people present — CO₂ rises with occupancy.

  3. A 0–10 V, 0–2,000 ppm CO₂ sensor reads 4.5 V. What is the CO₂?

    Show answer
    4.5 × 200 = 900 ppm.

  4. When does automatic baseline calibration give wrong readings?

    Show answer
    When the space never empties (24/7 occupancy), so the lowest reading over the period is not really outdoor air.

  5. What is the difference between CO and CO₂ sensors?

    Show answer
    CO₂ (carbon dioxide) indicates occupancy; CO (carbon monoxide) is a toxic exhaust gas, monitored in car parks. They use different sensors and limits.
