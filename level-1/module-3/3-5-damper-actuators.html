3.5 Damper actuators

Week 3 · Day 15About 10 minPage 5 of 8✓ Done
After this page you can
  • Tell opposed-blade and parallel-blade dampers apart and know where each is used.
  • Compare on/off, floating and modulating actuators.
  • Explain spring return, fail-safe positions, running time and torque.
  • Set up and troubleshoot a modulating damper actuator.
Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. Convert 36 m³/h to L/s.
    from 3.4 Water flow meters
    Show answer
    36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 L/s.
  2. Why do airflow probes need a straight duct run?
    from 3.3 Differential pressure and airflow sensors
    Show answer
    Bends, fans and dampers make the airflow uneven and turbulent, which gives jumpy or biased readings.
  3. Why is RH always measured together with temperature?
    from 3.2 Humidity and CO₂ sensors
    Show answer
    RH depends on temperature: the same air shows a higher RH when cooler. Without temperature you cannot interpret it or calculate dew point.
In one line

A damper actuator is a small motor that turns a damper's shaft to open, close or position it, in response to a control signal from the BMS.

?Why it matters

Dampers decide how much fresh air comes in, where air goes and whether a duct is open at all. A stuck or wrongly-set damper can starve a building of fresh air, waste energy, or let a fan run against a closed duct. Actuators are among the most frequently replaced field devices — and among the most often wired or set wrongly.

Fail-safe position

Choose what happens on power loss. A fresh air damper is usually spring-return closed so unfiltered, humid air cannot pour in when the AHU is off. Smoke-control dampers follow the fire strategy, not the BMS.

Setting up a modulating actuator

  1. Mount it on the damper shaft with the damper fully closed and the actuator at its 0 % end; tighten the shaft clamp.
  2. Set the rotation direction switch so that increasing signal opens the damper.
  3. Select the signal type (0–10 V or 2–10 V) to match the controller output.
  4. Wire supply, common, control signal (Y) and feedback (U). Many makers number these 1, 2, 3 and 5 — always check the label.
  5. Run the adaptation / stroke function if the actuator has one, so it learns the real end stops.
  6. Command 0, 50 and 100 % from the BMS and check the damper on site and the feedback each time.
Powering many actuators

Add up the VA of every actuator on a 24 V AC transformer (using the running figure) and add margin. Undersized transformers cause actuators to stall or behave erratically when several move at once. Keep 24 V polarity consistent: the controller's signal common and the actuator's supply common must be the same conductor.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01's fresh air damper uses a modulating, spring-return-closed actuator: 24 V AC, 0–10 V control from an AO. It sits at 20 % minimum when the AHU runs and closes on power loss or when the AHU stops. The return damper is a manual balancing damper, so it has no actuator.

⚒On site

  • Check the damper itself moves — actuators often turn while a loose shaft clamp lets the damper stay put.
  • Use the actuator's manual override (button or crank) to check the damper moves freely end to end.
  • Feedback different from command? The damper is binding, or the actuator has not been adapted.
  • Look for the position indicator on the actuator — it shows the actuator's position, not necessarily the damper's.

AKey terms

Opposed-blade damper
Adjacent blades rotate in opposite directions; good for modulating control.
Parallel-blade damper
All blades rotate the same way; used for open/closed duty.
Floating (3-point) control
Two outputs drive the actuator open or closed; position inferred from time.
Modulating control
Continuous position control from an analogue signal such as 0–10 V.
Spring return
Actuator spring drives to a safe position on power loss.
Running time
Time for the actuator to travel its full stroke.
Torque
Turning force of an actuator, in Nm.
Adaptation
Actuator function that learns the real end positions of the damper or valve.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

The BMS shows AHU-01's fresh air damper at 50 % and the actuator's position indicator agrees, but there is almost no fresh air coming in. What do you check first?

The actuator says 50 %, but the damper blades may not have moved. A loose clamp on the shaft is the classic cause. Look at the blades themselves.

  1. Which damper type is best for modulating control, and why?

    Show answer
    Opposed-blade, because its flow changes more evenly with position, giving smoother control.

  2. How many BMS outputs does a floating actuator need?

    Show answer
    Two digital outputs: one to drive open, one to drive close.

  3. What is the usual fail-safe position for a fresh air damper, and why?

    Show answer
    Closed (spring return), so unconditioned outdoor air does not enter when the AHU is off or loses power.

  4. What is actuator adaptation?

    Show answer
    A function where the actuator runs end to end to learn the real stroke of the damper or valve, so 0–100 % maps to the full real movement.

  5. Why must you add up actuator VA before choosing a transformer?

    Show answer
    So the transformer can supply all actuators moving at once without the voltage dropping and actuators stalling.
