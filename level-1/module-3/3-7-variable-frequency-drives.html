3.7 Variable frequency drives (VFD)

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. Why do most modern systems use 2-way valves?
    from 3.6 Chilled water valves and actuators
    Show answer
    System flow then varies with load, so pumps can slow down (saving energy) and return water stays warm (good ΔT).
  2. How many BMS outputs does a floating actuator need?
    from 3.5 Damper actuators
    Show answer
    Two digital outputs: one to drive open, one to drive close.
  3. What is a pulse weight, and why must it match?
    from 3.4 Water flow meters
    Show answer
    The quantity one pulse represents (e.g. 10 L). If the BMS uses a different value, all totals are wrong by that factor.
In one line

A VFD (variable frequency drive, also called an inverter or variable speed drive) changes a motor's speed by changing the frequency of its supply — so fans and pumps deliver only what is needed, using far less energy.

?Why it matters

Almost every new fan and pump in a commercial building has a VFD, and the fan laws (page 2.4) mean they save a lot of energy. For the BMS engineer a VFD is a busy interface: hard-wired start, status and speed signals plus a communication link — and several parameters that decide whether the BMS is actually in control.

⚙How it works

How it works

  1. The rectifier turns the incoming AC supply (e.g. 415 V, 50 Hz) into DC.
  2. The DC bus capacitors smooth and store that DC.
  3. The inverter switches the DC very fast to build a new AC supply at whatever frequency is required.
  4. Motor speed is proportional to frequency: 50 Hz = full speed, 25 Hz ≈ half speed.

Stored energy

The DC bus capacitors stay charged for minutes after isolation. Wait for the time printed on the drive (often 5–15 minutes) and check the DC bus has discharged before touching power terminals. Motor cables are screened; keep BMS signal cables away from them and land screens as the maker says, or the drive's switching noise will appear on your analogue signals.

Hand–Off–Auto on the keypad

Most drives have their own Hand/Off/Auto keys. In Hand the speed comes from the keypad; in Auto the BMS controls start and speed. A drive left in Hand after maintenance is a very common reason for “the BMS has lost control”. The drive's relay or a network value can report which mode it is in.

Bypass

Some critical fans and pumps have a bypass starter so the motor can run direct-on-line at full speed if the drive fails. The BMS should know which path is running (a DI from the bypass contactor).

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01's supply fan VFD gets: start from a DO via an interposing relay, speed from an AO (0–10 V = 0–50 Hz), and returns run and fault relays to DIs. Over Modbus RTU the BMS reads frequency, current, kW, kWh and the last fault code. Control source: terminals; minimum frequency 20 Hz; it normally runs at 42 Hz.

⚒On site

  • When the BMS “has no control”, look at the keypad first: Hand or Auto? Local or remote?
  • Check that the AI type on the drive (voltage or current, often a switch as well as a parameter) matches the BMS output.
  • Record the key parameters after commissioning; a drive replacement then takes minutes, not hours.
  • Compare the frequency on the drive display with what the BMS commands — a mismatch points at scaling.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

The BMS shows AHU-01's speed command at 100 %, but the VFD display shows 20 Hz and the fan is slow. Run status is ON. What is the most likely cause?

The drive is running (status ON) and obeying the start, but reads no useful speed reference, so it holds minimum frequency. Check the AI type (switch and parameter), the reference source and the voltage at the terminals.

  1. Explain in three steps how a VFD makes a variable-frequency supply.

    Show answer
    Rectifier converts AC to DC → DC bus stores and smooths it → inverter switches the DC to make AC at the required frequency.

  2. List four hard-wired signals between the BMS and a VFD.

    Show answer
    Start (DO), speed reference (AO), run status (DI), fault (DI).

  3. Why does a fan VFD have a minimum frequency?

    Show answer
    To keep enough airflow and motor cooling, and avoid running the fan too slowly to be useful.

  4. What is the danger of the DC bus after isolation?

    Show answer
    The capacitors remain charged for several minutes; touching power terminals before they discharge can be lethal.

  5. The drive ignores the BMS start command. Name two settings to check.

    Show answer
    Whether the keypad is in Hand/local, and the control (command) source parameter.
