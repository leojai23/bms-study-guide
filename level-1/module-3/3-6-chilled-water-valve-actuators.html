3.6 Chilled water valves and actuators

Week 3 · Day 16About 10 minPage 6 of 8✓ Done

In one line

A control valve and its actuator decide how much chilled water flows through a coil — and so how much cooling it gives. Choosing, sizing and setting them up correctly is what makes a temperature loop stable.

?Why it matters

The valve is the final control element of nearly every cooling loop. An oversized valve makes the loop hunt; a leaking one wastes energy and causes low ΔT; the wrong fail-safe can freeze a coil or flood a room with cold air. Many “PID tuning problems” are really valve problems.

Sizing: Kv

Valve flow coefficient

Kv = flow in m³/h through the fully open valve with a 1 bar pressure drop.

Flow (m³/h) = Kv × √ΔP (bar)  →  Kv = Flow ÷ √ΔP

Example: a coil needs 9 m³/h and the valve should take 0.25 bar → Kv = 9 ÷ 0.5 = 18. Choose the nearest standard Kvs at or just below.

A valve must take a decent share of the circuit's pressure drop to control well. This share is its authority: ΔP(valve) ÷ [ΔP(valve) + ΔP(rest of the circuit)]. Aim for about 0.5. A valve with low authority does nothing for most of its stroke, then everything at once.

Why equal-percentage?

A cooling coil gives a large part of its capacity at a small part of its flow. If the valve were linear, the first 20 % of opening would deliver most of the cooling and the loop would be twitchy at low load. An equal-percentage valve does the opposite, so the combined valve-plus-coil response is roughly linear — and the PID loop behaves the same at every load.

Pressure-independent valves

A PICV holds the flow for a given opening constant even when pump pressure changes — so when other valves close, this coil is not suddenly over-supplied. Its maximum flow is set with a dial, replacing a separate balancing valve. It needs a minimum ΔP across it (from the datasheet) to work.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01's CHW valve is a 2-way, equal-percentage valve with a modulating actuator (24 V AC, 0–10 V control from an AO). On power loss it stays where it is (non-spring-return). The program closes it whenever the fan is off, so the coil does not get cold and sweat when there is no airflow.

⚒On site

  • Look at the flow arrow on the valve body — globe valves fitted backwards chatter and leak.
  • Check the actuator is fully coupled: command 0 % and 100 % and watch the stem or indicator move the full stroke.
  • To test for passing (leaking), command 0 % with the AHU running: supply air and the pipe after the valve should warm up.
  • Note the PICV's maximum-flow dial setting and check it against the balancing schedule.

AKey terms

2-way valve
Valve that varies flow through the coil; system flow varies.
3-way valve
Valve that diverts unused flow through a bypass; system flow stays constant.
Kv / Kvs
Flow (m³/h) at 1 bar drop; Kvs is the value when fully open.
Valve authority
Share of circuit pressure drop taken by the valve; about 0.5 is good.
Equal-percentage
Valve characteristic where each step of opening increases flow by the same percentage.
PICV
Pressure-independent control valve — flow set by opening, unaffected by system pressure.
Close-off pressure
Maximum ΔP against which a valve and actuator can shut tight.
Passing valve
A valve that leaks flow when commanded closed.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

AHU-01's return air temperature swings ±1.5 °C every few minutes at light load, but is steady at full load. The valve was replaced last month with a much larger one “to be safe”. What is the likely cause?

An oversized valve works in the bottom few percent of its stroke at low load, where small movements make large changes — the loop hunts. Size the valve to the coil (Kv), or use a PICV.

  1. Why do most modern systems use 2-way valves?

    Show answer
    System flow then varies with load, so pumps can slow down (saving energy) and return water stays warm (good ΔT).

  2. A coil needs 4 m³/h with a 0.16 bar valve drop. What Kv is needed?

    Show answer
    Kv = 4 ÷ √0.16 = 4 ÷ 0.4 = 10.

  3. Why are equal-percentage valves used on coils?

    Show answer
    Coils give most of their output at low flow; an equal-percentage valve compensates, so the combined response is close to linear and the loop is stable at every load.

  4. What does close-off pressure mean?

    Show answer
    The maximum pressure difference against which the valve and actuator can close tightly; above it the valve passes.

  5. What is the advantage of a PICV?

    Show answer
    Its flow depends only on its opening, not on changes in system pressure, so coils are not over- or under-supplied as other valves move; it also replaces the balancing valve.
