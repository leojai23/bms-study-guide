3.4 Water flow meters

  • Configure a pulse output and convert between flow units.

Warm-up · 3 quick questions from earlier pages (2 min)
  1. Where are the ports of a duct static pressure sensor connected?
    from 3.3 Differential pressure and airflow sensors
    Show answer
    + to a tap inside the duct, − open to the surroundings (plant room or ceiling space).
  2. When does automatic baseline calibration give wrong readings?
    from 3.2 Humidity and CO₂ sensors
    Show answer
    When the space never empties (24/7 occupancy), so the lowest reading over the period is not really outdoor air.
  3. Where should the tip of an immersion sensor sit?
    from 3.1 Temperature sensors (NTC, PT1000)
    Show answer
    In the middle third of the pipe, in a thermowell filled with thermal paste.
In one line

Flow meters measure how much water passes through a pipe; add a pair of temperature sensors and a calculator and you have an energy (BTU) meter that tells you how much cooling was delivered.

?Why it matters

Flow and energy data turn a BMS from a control system into an energy-management system: you can see what each plant item or tenant really uses, bill for it, and spot waste such as low ΔT. Meters are also used to prove flow before starting chillers. They are expensive, and a badly installed one is worse than none — it gives confident, wrong numbers.

Straight pipe

Bends, valves and pumps disturb the flow profile. Meters need straight pipe upstream and downstream — for magmeters typically about 5 pipe diameters before and 2–3 after; clamp-on ultrasonic meters usually need more. Use the maker's figures.

Energy (BTU) meters

Cooling power

kW = 4.19 × flow (L/s) × ΔT (K)

Example: 5 L/s with 7 °C in and 12.5 °C out → 4.19 × 5 × 5.5 ≈ 115 kW. Over 10 hours that is 1,150 kWh of cooling.

The two temperature sensors are a matched pair (often PT500 or PT1000), calibrated together, because a small error in ΔT gives a big error in energy: on a 5.5 K ΔT, a 0.1 K error is about 2 %.

Getting data out of the meter

OutputHow the BMS uses it
4–20 mA / 0–10 VInstantaneous flow (or power) on an AI — fine for control, no totals.
Pulse outputEach pulse = a fixed volume or energy (e.g. 1 pulse = 10 L). The BMS counts pulses on a DI to build totals.
Modbus, BACnet, M-BusEverything: flow, temperatures, power, totals, alarms. Best for energy monitoring.

When counting pulses, set the pulse weight (litres or kWh per pulse) in the BMS to match the meter, and check the input can count fast enough at maximum flow.

Flow switches

Where you only need to know that water is flowing — chiller and pump proving — a flow switch (paddle or thermal) closes a contact above a set flow. It is a DI, and part of the chiller's safety interlocks.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 has no meter of its own, but the 3rd floor has an energy meter on its chilled water branch, read over Modbus, so the floor's cooling energy can be compared with AHU-01's valve position and run-hours. A floor using more energy for the same weather and occupancy is a sign of a fault.

⚒On site

  • Check the flow-direction arrow on the meter body — reversed meters read zero or negative.
  • Magmeters must be in a pipe that is always full: not at a high point where air collects.
  • Check which pipe each temperature sensor is in. Swapped sensors give negative energy.
  • Compare pulse totals with the meter's own display over a few days to prove the pulse weight.

AKey terms

Magmeter
Electromagnetic flow meter for conductive liquids; no moving parts.
Ultrasonic meter
Flow meter using the transit time of sound waves with and against the flow.
Energy (BTU) meter
Flow meter + matched temperature pair + calculator that totals thermal energy.
Matched pair
Two temperature sensors calibrated together for an accurate ΔT.
Pulse weight
Quantity represented by one output pulse, e.g. 10 L or 1 kWh.
M-Bus
Meter-Bus: a standard for reading utility meters.
Flow switch
Device that closes a contact when flow exceeds a set value.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

A new BTU meter on the 3rd-floor branch shows −110 kW during the working day. Flow looks right. What is wrong?

With a correct flow, a negative power of a believable size means the meter sees the return as colder than the supply: the sensors are swapped (or the configuration says the wrong pipe).

  1. Why does a magmeter need a full pipe?

    Show answer
    It measures the voltage across the flowing water; with air in the pipe the measurement is wrong or lost.

  2. Calculate the cooling for 8 L/s with a 5 K ΔT.

    Show answer
    4.19 × 8 × 5 ≈ 168 kW.

  3. What is a pulse weight, and why must it match?

    Show answer
    The quantity one pulse represents (e.g. 10 L). If the BMS uses a different value, all totals are wrong by that factor.

  4. Convert 36 m³/h to L/s.

    Show answer
    36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 L/s.

  5. Why do energy meters use a matched temperature pair?

    Show answer
    Because energy depends on a small ΔT; matched sensors keep the ΔT error tiny.
