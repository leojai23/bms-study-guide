3.3 Differential pressure and airflow sensors

In one line

A differential pressure (ΔP) sensor measures the difference in pressure between its two ports — across a filter, between a duct and a room, or between two points in an airflow probe to work out velocity.

?Why it matters

ΔP sensors are everywhere in HVAC: they tell you when filters are dirty, they control VAV fans, they prove fans are moving air, they keep clean rooms at the right pressure and they measure airflow. They are simple devices — but a swapped or loose tube gives readings that send whole systems the wrong way.

⚙How it works

Two ports, one difference

A ΔP sensor has a high (+) and a low (−) port, each connected by plastic tube to a measuring point. It outputs the difference in pascals (Pa), as 0–10 V, 4–20 mA, or over a bus. A ΔP switch does the same job but only closes a contact above a set pressure.

Units: 1 Pa is tiny — 100 Pa = 1 mbar; 1 kPa = 1,000 Pa; 1 bar = 100 kPa. Many ΔP sensors have a selectable range (DIP switch or menu) — the controller's scaling must match the range selected.

Static, total and velocity pressure

Air velocity from velocity pressure (air at about 1.2 kg/m³)

v ≈ 1.29 × √Pv (m/s, with Pv in Pa)  ·  Airflow = v × duct area

Example: Pv = 100 Pa → v ≈ 1.29 × 10 = 12.9 m/s. In a 0.5 × 0.4 m duct (0.2 m²): 12.9 × 0.2 ≈ 2.6 m³/s (2,600 L/s).

Straight runs

Bends, dampers and fans make the airflow uneven. Airflow probes and duct static sensors need a stretch of straight duct before them — the maker states how much. Poor placement gives jumpy or biased readings.

Follow AHU-01

AHU-01 has two pressure devices: a filter ΔP switch (contact closes when the filter is dirty → DI) and a supply duct static pressure sensor, 0–500 Pa, 4–20 mA (→ AI), with its + port in the duct and its − port open to the plant room. Lab 4 wires and scales this sensor.

⚒On site

  • Check the tubes: + to the higher pressure, − to the lower. Swapped tubes give negative readings, or zero on sensors that cannot read negative.
  • Run tubes without kinks or sags where condensate can collect; use rigid connectors through the duct wall.
  • Most ΔP sensors have a zero (auto-zero) function — use it with both ports open to the same pressure during commissioning.
  • Check the range jumper or menu against the controller's scaling.

AKey terms

Differential pressure (ΔP)
Difference in pressure between two points.
Static pressure
Pressure the air exerts on the duct walls.
Total pressure
Static pressure plus velocity pressure, seen by a probe facing the flow.
Velocity pressure
Pressure due to air movement: total minus static.
Pitot tube
Probe facing the airflow that measures total (and often static) pressure.
K-factor
Constant relating ΔP to flow for a specific probe or box: flow = K × √ΔP.
ΔP switch
Device that closes a contact when the pressure difference exceeds a setpoint.
Pa (pascal)
Unit of pressure; 100 Pa = 1 mbar.

✓Check yourself

Try each one before opening the answer.

Fault scenario · what would you do?

A new filter ΔP sensor on AHU-03 reads −120 Pa while the fan runs. What has happened?

Air pressure is higher before the filter than after it, so + must go before the filter. A negative reading of a sensible size means the tubes are swapped.

  1. What do the + and − ports of a ΔP sensor mean?

    Show answer
    + is the high-pressure port and − the low-pressure port; the output is the + pressure minus the − pressure.

  2. Calculate air velocity for a velocity pressure of 64 Pa.

    Show answer
    v ≈ 1.29 × √64 = 1.29 × 8 ≈ 10.3 m/s.

  3. Where are the ports of a duct static pressure sensor connected?

    Show answer
    + to a tap inside the duct, − open to the surroundings (plant room or ceiling space).

  4. Name four uses of ΔP sensors or switches in HVAC.

    Show answer
    Any four of: filter monitoring, duct static pressure control, fan proving, room pressure, airflow measurement, water ΔP for pumps.

  5. Why do airflow probes need a straight duct run?

    Show answer
    Bends, fans and dampers make the airflow uneven and turbulent, which gives jumpy or biased readings.
