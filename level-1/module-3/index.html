Level 1 · Module 3

Sensors and Field Devices

The devices that measure and move: sensors, meters, actuators, drives and status switches.

Field devices are where the BMS meets the physical building. This module covers what each common sensor and actuator measures or moves, how to choose and mount it, the signals it uses, and how it fails. Two interactive tools help: the NTC/PT1000 calculator on page 3.1, and the scaling calculator you will meet in Module 4.

Module 3 quiz · question 1 of 10

What happens to a PT1000's resistance as temperature rises?

PT1000: 1,000 Ω at 0 °C, rising about 3.85 Ω/°C. (See page 3.1.)
Module 3 quiz · question 2 of 10

An NTC sensor input suddenly reads −40 °C. Most likely cause?

Open circuit → infinite resistance → an NTC reads very cold. (See page 3.1.)
Module 3 quiz · question 3 of 10

A 0–10 V CO₂ sensor scaled 0–2,000 ppm reads 5 V. CO₂ is…

5 V × 200 ppm/V = 1,000 ppm. (See page 3.2.)
Module 3 quiz · question 4 of 10

Why can automatic baseline calibration fail in a hospital ward?

ABC assumes the lowest recent reading is outdoor air. (See page 3.2.)
Module 3 quiz · question 5 of 10

A filter ΔP sensor reads −90 Pa with the fan running. What is wrong?

Pressure is higher before the filter; + belongs there. (See page 3.3.)
Module 3 quiz · question 6 of 10

Velocity pressure is 100 Pa. Air velocity is about…

v ≈ 1.29 × √100 = 12.9 m/s. (See page 3.3.)
Module 3 quiz · question 7 of 10

An energy meter reads negative power with correct flow. Most likely?

Return reading colder than supply gives negative energy. (See page 3.4.)
Module 3 quiz · question 8 of 10

A floating (3-point) actuator needs…

One output drives open, the other drives close. (See page 3.5.)
Module 3 quiz · question 9 of 10

Why are equal-percentage valves used on cooling coils?

Valve + coil together give a near-linear response. (See page 3.6.)
Module 3 quiz · question 10 of 10

A belt breaks. Which status device will still say the fan is running?

The contactor stays pulled in even though no air moves. (See page 3.8.)
